## Summary
Interlocking patents, when very minute pieces of information are able to be patented, stalls innovation rather than promoting it. This is because new inventions are built off of old ones, and if old technology is continually patented, it leaves no room for new innovation.
## Significance
This is a step in Chang’s argument against the strict patent and IPR laws that are in place.

## Explaination
I think that there are some holes with this argument. Chang doesn’t specify what he means when he says that a piece of information is “minute.” Who is to say whether Amazon’s one click purchase is too small of an invention to patent? When does an idea become no longer minute?
