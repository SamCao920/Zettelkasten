## Summary
The idea that one’s suffering is brought upon ultimately by oneself. A major theme in works such as Hamlet, and the Greek Tragedies.
## Significance
This has been a common theme for many works of philosophy and literature throughout the ages and is a crucial aspect of the human condition as explored through works of literature.
## Examples
- This is a prominent theme in *Hamlet*, as Hamlet is forced to suffer through the murder of his father, and his own indecision, being unable to take revenge.
	- This indecision is likely due to several factors, including his unsureness about the nature of the ghost that informs him of the murder of his father, his intellectualism (he was a scholar at the University of Wittenburg) leading to over-analyzation and paralysis of action, and his psychological complexity, related to his potential Oedipus complex.
- This is likewise a prominent theme in the Sophoclean tragedies, for example, *Oedipus the King*, where Oedipus is forced advance his own down fall unknowingly, suffering all the way along.
- This is also the case with both Victor and the creature throughout *Frankenstein*. 
	- Victor is forced to constantly suffer the result of his own creation, his creature, as the creature terrorizes him and murders the ones that he loves. 
	- At the same time, the creature feels unloved due to his appearance, turning to violence as a means of attempting to achieve catharsis with his inner torment. Yet, this violence does not serve to mitigate his suffering; rather, it worsens his suffering tormenting him with remorse.
