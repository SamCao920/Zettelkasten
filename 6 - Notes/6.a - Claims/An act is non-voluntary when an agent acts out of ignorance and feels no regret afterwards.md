## Summary
An act is considered to be “non-voluntary” when an agent acts out of ignorance, but does not feel regret for the action afterwards.
## Significance
The distinction between “non-voluntary” actions and “involuntary” actions are slim, and this distinction made by Aristotle is often confused.
## Explanation
A non-voluntary action would be if, for example, an agent were to step on someone else’s toes without realizing it, but then showing no reaction at all.
Aristotle considers this to be neither morally repugnant (since the agent did not commit the action willingly), but nor does he consider such an action morally praiseworthy.
