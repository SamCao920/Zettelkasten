## Summary
Dostoevsky argues that suffering can be good, and lead to a spiritual transformation, if it is done correctly. If done incorrectly, it can trap someone.
## Significance
This is a major theme that is explicated in *TBK*.
## Explanation
Dostoevsky distinguishes between two forms of suffering:
- **Lamentation**: “true suffering” that has a genuine cause that originates in the world. If one has faith and openness to the “web of connectedness,” lamentations can serve to purify us. However, if it is treated badly and allowed to fester, it can become the other form of suffering.
- **Lacerations:** suffering to feel morally superior to others, or because one feels guilt tripped into continuing to suffer. Dostoevsky considers this to be the wrong kind of suffering, and since it is centered on the self and pride, it leads to isolation.
In order for suffering to “absolve one of sin,” one must go through a process of **expiation,** which means accepting the suffering and not avoiding it, for instance, through confession.
Dimitri is the example that is used in TBK to show that at the end of suffering, there is light and joy. One just has to suffering in the right way.
