## Summary
Perhaps one of Aristotle’s most impactful and famous ideas, the idea that to be morally virtuous is to act in accordance with the golden mean, which means not acting with deficiency nor with excess. Additionally, to be virtuous means to be in the condition of being in the mean, so the mean cannot simply be a passing impulse.
## Significance
This is one of Aristotle’s most significant moral ideas, and the central pillar of his moral philosophy.
## Explanation
The mean is something that is circumstantial and not absolute. In other words, it is ordinal, not cardinal. It varies based on the time, location, people involved, etc.
In some things, the mean is the “extreme.” this has two meanings:
1. The very fact that some action is virtuous means that it is extreme on the “virtuous-immoral” spectrum, given that is the most virtuous things that you can do.
2. Additionally, there are some things that are inherently bad, and there is no “mean” to reach for those things, including spite, envy, adultery, theft, murder, etc.
