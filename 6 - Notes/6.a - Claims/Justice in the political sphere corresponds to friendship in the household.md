## Summary
Aristotle compares political systems (particularly with respect to their treatment of justice) and analogizes them to situations within a household. In doing so, he finds similarities between the two.
## Significance
This is important for understanding how to approach friendships within a household.
## Explanation
- **Monarchy**: *father-son*; the father rules over the son like a benevolent king, having absolute authority but exercising it for the benefit of the son.
- **Aristocracy**: *husband-wife*; the most natural relationship, where each person focuses on their strengths, where they rule the household based on their respective merit in different tasks.
- **Timocracy**: *brother-brother*; the most equal relationship, the closest to a friendship of virtue found between unrelated peers (since the brothers are roughly equal, have a shared history, and if one is good the other is also likely good since they were raised together).
The first two are virtue friendships too, but of secondary quality, since they lack equality.
Additionally, an implication is that since there are “corruptions” of these forms of government (tyranny, oligarchy, and democracy respectively), the relations in the family can likewise be corrupted. The worse case of such an corruption is tyranny in the household, which is also, in regards to political systems, the worst form of corruption.
