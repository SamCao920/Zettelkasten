## Summary
Often times, activist organizations will switch focus after their initial stated goals have been competed in order to continue functioning. This often leads such groups to be detrimental overall to society, rather than helping society.
## Significance
This means that often times, these organizations can stray away from their original goals and support causes that may actually be harmful.
## Explanation
This is known as the “March of Dimes” syndrome.
Many activist groups in history, once they have successfully achieved their goals, shifted to oftentimes even counterproductive goals in order to continue operating.
## Examples
- **The March of Dimes itself** — Founded to combat polio, it achieved its goal through vaccines in the 1950s but kept going by shifting its mission to preventing birth defects.
- **Antiwar activists turning antinuclear** — Veterans of the Vietnam War protest movement pivoted to leading the anti-nuclear power movement after the Three Mile Island accident in 1979, with figures like Jane Fonda and Tom Hayden at the forefront.
- **The LGBTQ+ rights movement** — After achieving major wins (decriminalization of homosexuality, same-sex marriage legalization in 2015, civil rights protections in 2020), activists declared a "national state of emergency" in 2023 and pushed for a range of new and more controversial causes.
- **The civil rights movement / NAACP** — After landmark legislative victories (Civil Rights Act of 1964, Voting Rights Act of 1965), the movement shifted from demanding equality to demanding affirmative action, reparations, and other causes that contradicted its original integration-focused mission.
- **The Southern Poverty Law Center (SPLC)** — Launched to represent individual discrimination victims, it pivoted to suing the KKK, then to publishing expanding lists of "hate groups" that critics say include mainstream conservative organizations, all as a fund-raising strategy.
- **Black Lives Matter** — Emerged and grew into a massive movement despite the rarity of the specific type of deaths it ostensibly addressed, and critics argue its antipolice posture contributed to more Black deaths via the "Ferguson Effect."
- **The #MeToo movement** — Rose to prominence during a period when rates of sexual violence had actually fallen nearly 60% (from 1995 to 2010), and came after corporate America had already instituted strict harassment protections.
- **Antismoking activists opposing vaping** — After smoking rates dropped sharply thanks to nicotine vaping, antismoking groups like the Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids manufactured scares about vaping to maintain relevance and funding, despite vaping being widely recognized as far less harmful than tobacco.
- **Environmental/climate activists** — After previous doomsday predictions (overpopulation, energy scarcity, a synthetic-chemical "cancer epidemic") proved false, green activists escalated to claiming humanity faces an "existential threat" from climate change.
- **Jane Fonda specifically** — Highlighted as a special case ("the March of Jane syndrome"): she helped kill the nuclear power industry through activism, which caused a switch to coal and increased carbon emissions — and then became a leading climate activist fighting the very problem her earlier activism helped create.
