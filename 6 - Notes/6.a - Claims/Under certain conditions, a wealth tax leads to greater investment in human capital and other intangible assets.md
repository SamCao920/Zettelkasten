## Summary
A wealth tax may create an incentive to shelter wealth in the firm, leading to greater intangible asset and human capital investments, though this does not apply to physical capital investments.
## Significance
This is a potential argument in favor of establishing a wealth tax, given the right kind of barriers and regulations.
## Explanation
Obviously, this tax would only be effective if place upon business owners that have the ability to readily investment their capital.
This effect is only present when the tax represents less than 5% of liquid assets of the taxpayer. If the tax is increased to more than 20%, then the effect becomes negative as owners begin to extract capital from their business in order to pay the tax.
For the average firm owner in Norway, the portfolio reallocation effect (i.e., investing more into the firm) is stronger than the liquidity constraint (i.e., taking capital out of the business).
The wealth tax also means that owners will work more, since they fell like they are relatively poorer.
