## Summary
Light-dependent reactions, which consists of the activities carried out by the Photosystem II, E.T.C., and Photosystem I, generates energy in the form of ATP and NADPH.
## Significance
This is one of the main parts of photosynthesis, as it contributes the energy needed for the Calvin cycle to occur.
## Explanation
The following are the steps of a light-dependent reaction:
1. The chlorophyll in Photosystem II absorbs light energy, and the electrons inside are excited, so they move out of the chlorophyll and into the E.T.C.
2. Photolysis occurs in Photosystem II, meaning that water is oxidized, resulting in the creation of hydrogen ions (which contribute to the proton gradient), and electrons, which replace the lost electrons in the chlorophyll. Oxygen is released as a byproduct.
3. As the electron moves down the E.T.C, it powers a proton pump, which contributes to the proton gradient, powering the ATP synthase, generating ATP. This process is known as photophosphorylation.
4. The electron moves to Photosystem I, where it is reenergized by sunlight and given to a NADP+, after which the NADPH moves to into the stoma where it is used as a part of the Calvin cycle.
