## Summary
Most systems, when there are internal imbalances, will tend towards equilibrium due to some equalizing force within the system.
## Significance
This seems to be an inductive rule regard the many systems that exist across many fields, and could be used as a conceptual framework to predict the outcomes of yet-to-exist systems.
## Explanation
This is something that is observable throughout all different fields, namely the principle that when an external force attempts to impose some change on a system, the system will internally readjust such that the system returns to its original state.
For instance, this is seen through:
- Le Chatelier's Principle
## Counterexamples
- The one-child policy made irreversible changes to China's population dynamics.
## Note
Perhaps the reason that some systems don't actually settle into a stable equilibrium is because not all systems have a stable equilibrium. Some systems may be have unstable equilibriums whereby once the system is disrupted, the system collapses to a completely different state.
