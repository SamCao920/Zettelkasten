## Summary
It is impossible for someone to voluntarily choose to be treated unjustly. To be treated unjustly is a bad thing. And since no one truly wishes for what is bad for them, no one can voluntarily accept injustice. Also, justice must be conducted with more than one person present.
## Significance
This is a crucial argument for Aristotle to make, since otherwise, there is a contradictory possibility for someone to be unjust to themselves (for instance, through giving themselves too little when it comes to distributive justice; in such a case the person is gaining something else, perhaps honor).
## Note
Committing suicide, which seems to be an injustice against oneself, is indeed an injustice against the state when viewed through the lens of legal justice.
Metaphorically, parts of one’s soul is able to engage unjustly with other parts of the soul, though this is not true “justice” and “injustice” in the sense used elsewhere.
