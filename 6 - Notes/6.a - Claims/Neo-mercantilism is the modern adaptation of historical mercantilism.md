## Summary
Whereas historical mercantilism had a single-minded focus on maintaining a trade surplus and accumulating precious materials, neo-mercantilism is more complex, as it integrates capitalistic elements. 
## Significance
This is an important distinct to make, since many countries today are considered to be “neo-mercantilist,” though this does not mean that they are mercantilist in the 16-17th century sense.

## Explaination
**Neo-mercantilism:** 
- Focuses on national economic strength.
- Favoring exports over imports, though with nuance.
- State-guided capitalism.
- Focus on industrial policy; technology, infrastructure, and innovation are prioritized.
- State-subsidized/directed industries (which are considered to be crucial for the state).
	- Low domestic competition.
**Mercantilism:**
- Resource extraction from colonies that also serve as markets for the mother state.
- Heavy economic regulation and government monopolies.
- Hoarding of precious mineral wealth.
- Maximize export and minimize imports.
