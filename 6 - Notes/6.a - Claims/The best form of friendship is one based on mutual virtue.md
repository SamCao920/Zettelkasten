## Summary
A friendship that is based on mutual virtue is both long lasting and fruitful.
## Significance
Aristotle considers this form of friendship to the best of all of the types of friendships.
## Explanation
Friendships based on mutual virtue are perfect. 
- They last for a long time, since virtue is a disposition and dispositions are not easily changed.
- They take time to develop, since two people need to prove to each other that they are deserving of love.
	- A person ought to be loved by another both universally (generally, absolutely, based on general goodness/pleasantness) and contingently (good/pleasant for themself).
- They encompass both the friendship of utility and of pleasure, since good people are both useful and pleasant to other good people.
- This is the only type of friendship where there is true selflessness, where you wish good for the friend for the friend’s sake, not for personal gain.
	- This connects back to the principle Aristotle states, which is that it is more important to love than to be loved in a friendship.
	- When one wishes well for a friend, it is out of a moral state (disposition), not just a passing feeling.
- These types of friendships are rare.
