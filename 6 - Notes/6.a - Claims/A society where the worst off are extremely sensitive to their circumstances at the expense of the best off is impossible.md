## Summary
There is a possible society where the prospects of the worst off can be improved marginally, but the prospects of all those better are extremely negatively affected. This seems hardly acceptable, and yet, would be supported by Rawls’ views. Rawls argues that this is impossible.
## Significance
This is a common and powerful objection against Rawls’ views.
## Counterargument
Rawls argues that this is simply an inconceivable situation, since with realistic institutional assumptions, this situation could never occur. Additionally, with an open and competitive economy, social mobility mitigates this issue.
