## Summary
Atomic sentences are independent in propositional logic, meaning that the truth of one atom does not force the truth of another.
## Significance
This is due to the invisible nature of atoms, since their contents are irrelevant for their logical use, the truth of one statement cannot determine the truth value of another statement.
## Example
Given that $P$ stands for the ball is entirely red, and $Q$ stands for the ball is entirely green, both statement can stand as-is in a logical statement, until some explicit declaration is made otherwise.
