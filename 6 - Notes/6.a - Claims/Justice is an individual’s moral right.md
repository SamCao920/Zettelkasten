## Summary
Mill’s ultimate definition of justice is that it is someone’s moral right, and it represents the rules of conduct applicable to all and intended for the good of all. Justice is a perfect duty.
## Significance
This is Mill’s definition of justice.
## Explanation
Justice is essentially derived from the natural desire for retaliation when some social harm has been done. Importantly, the harm must be social (involving the general interest), or else, the retaliation is no longer a form of justice.
Injustice occurs when:
1. Someone is deprived of their liberty, property, or any other such lawful thing.
2. Taking something away from someone where that thing is a moral right.
3. One does not obtain what they deserve.
4. One breaks faith (a promise).
5. One is partial when one ought not to be.
When there are conflicting claims of justice, utilitarian calculus is used to resolve the tension. For instance, if we must steal in order to save a life, the utilitarian calculus would allow for the stealing in order to preserve someone’s life.
