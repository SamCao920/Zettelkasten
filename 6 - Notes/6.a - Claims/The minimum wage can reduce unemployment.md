## Summary
While traditional economic logic suggests that having a minimum wage reduces unemployment because some people whose productivity are below the minimum won’t be hired, theoretically, the claim could be made that minimum wage in fact increase employment.
## Significance
This challenges the view of the traditional economist and traditional economic logic, which suggests that the minimum wage actually raises employment rather than reduce it.
## Explanation
Say there are two people, John and Jack, the former ready to accept a wage of $10 and the latter $15. Assuming that the firm can hire only at a standard wage, and the revenue per worker is $16. 
1. In a case without minimum wage, the firm would only hire John, since they make a profit of $6 at a wage for John of $10. 
2. In a case where the minimum wage is $15, the firm would rationally hire both John and Jack, since at that point, adding an additional worker only increases their revenue, not decrease it. I.e., hiring just John means that the firm would make $1 of profit, while hiring both John and Jack means that the firm would make $2 of profit.
Therefore, there is a situation whereby having a minimum wage actually increases employment.
