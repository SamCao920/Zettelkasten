## Summary
Health care insurance providers are not the main cause of the high prices of US health insurance. Rather, the high cost is due to a combination of the providers and the AMA.
## Significance
This provides an alternative argument for the reason that health care costs are so high in the US.
## Explanation
There are several things which point to the fact that the reason for the high cost of health care is not the insurance companies:
- The profits of insurance companies are low compared to the average in the S&P 500, with the largest insurance company having a profit margin of 6%, and the second largest having between 2 and 4%. The average profit margin of a company on the S&P 500 is around double that.
	- The paltry margin means that if the largest insurance company, UnitedHealth Group, were to donate every single dollar of its profits to buying Americans more health care, they would only be able to buy 9% more.
	- Even if we were to look at the return on equity, the ROE of health insurance companies are still lower than that of the general population of companies in the S&P 500. Regardless, the financial success of these companies don’t matter, since neither way their small profit margins means that they can’t pay out that much extra health insurance anyways.
- The majority of the cost comes from the higher cost by providers.
	- The doctors and hospitals that actually provide care at the largest driver of health care costs, which demand some government intervention in order to negotiate the lower cost of drugs (through if done poorly, this may reduce innovation).
	- Due to the litigious nature of the US health care system, doctors will practice defensive medicine even in ambiguous situation. 
## Note
Americans actually pay a smaller amount out-of-pocket compared to many other rich countries.
