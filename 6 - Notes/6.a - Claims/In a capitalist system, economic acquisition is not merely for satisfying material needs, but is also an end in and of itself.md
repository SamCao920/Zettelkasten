## Summary
Under a capitalist system, economic acquisition is not undertaken merely as a means to satisfy some need. It is also (largely) taken as an end in and of itself.

## Significance
This is part of Weber’s argument in The Protestant Work Ethic. 

## Explaination
I think that while this is true to a certain extent, “in and of itself” is too overarching of a statement to me. More accurately, it would be that capitalism incentives economic acquisition as a means of providing more economic acquisition in the future (the accumulated wealth being thought of as economic capital). In a sense, Weber is right that the accumulation of capital can be thought of as being in and of itself (since capital is useless on its own), though this explanation makes it more clear.
