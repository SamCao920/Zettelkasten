## Summary
Mackinder’s Heartland Thesis argued that whoever controlled the heartland (see below, defined as the “Pivot Area”) would be able to control the Rimlands (defined below as the “Inner or marginal crescent”), and thereby, they would be able to control the entire world.
## Significance
This is a significant theory from international relations and realpolitik that informed many later theories regarding geopolitics.

## Explaination
![[Pasted image 20250922213419 1.png]]
I think that the theory carries some weight, though some historical evidence suggests that the state that controls the Pivot Area is not guaranteed to be able to control the entire world. I think that this weakens the theory, since a theory could really be produced about any region of the world, with the result that that specific region has some specific trait that allows it to conquer the world. That being said, there is other historical evidence to support the theory, including many of the nomadic conquerors.
It seems that the most important factor in determining the effectiveness of the conquests of the state in the pivot area is its access to transportation technology that is advanced and flexible. In that way, this theory could be considered “technological” more than “geographical.”
