## Summary
As the environment of the post-war consensus as highly conducive to the development of labor unions, as labor unions grew more numerous and larger, they began to have a chokehold over the economy and slow down its productivity growth.
## Significance
This explains why the UK shifted to such radical neoliberalism in the 1980s, since this socialist approach to management simple had not produced results.
## Effect of Labor Unions
There was very little productivity growth compared to the other European nations and low levels of investment in the economy. The UK became termed the “Sick Man of Europe,”
This reached a nadir from 1973-1979, when a combination of supply-side shocks, inflation, and labor union strikes wiped out British economics growth. During the Winter of Discontent (late 1978-early 1979), the situation was at its worse.
> [!QUOTE] Mark Kesselman, *Introduction to Comparative Politics*
> It seemed as ifeveryone was on strike. Strikes by truckers disrupted fuel supplies. Strikes by train drivers disrupted intercity commerce and visits to Granny. Some ambulance drivers refused to respond to emergency calls. Gravediggers refused to bury the dead.
