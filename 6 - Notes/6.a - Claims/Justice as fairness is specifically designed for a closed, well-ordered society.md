## Summary
Rawls' theory of justice is specifically designed without the consideration of private associations or international relations. Additionally, it assumes a well-ordered society, which he considers an idealization of actual societies.
## Significance
These are the assumptions that Rawls uses, and thus they can be challenged to challenge the entirety of his theory.
## Explanation
He makes these assumptions in order so that once a theory based on the assumptions have been established, the theory can more easily be expanded outwards to cover more intellectual ground. 
He does not claim that this theory holds for all cases.
