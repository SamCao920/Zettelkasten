## Summary
Because, under a MMP system, half of the seats in government are filled by people directly elected by their local constituency, the government of MMP is able to have a moral local outlook than strict PR, which typically has a more national perspective.
## Significance
This explains the advantage that MMP has over PR.
## Explanation
- Advantages of both over SMP:
	- Cannot be gerrymandered.
	- Prevents minority rule.
	- Allows for political diversity.
- Under MMP, a voter casts two votes: a vote for a representative, and a vote for a party. 
	- For the vote for the representative, the representative who receives the most vote wins, and becomes the MP for that area. All of the MPs here comprise half of the parliament.
	- For the vote for the party, the other half of parliament is made up of MPs from the parties, with the seats divided based on the portions of votes that they received.
		- The use of the “Party List,” a list of preferred candidates to receive the seats allocated for a particular party increases party loyalty.
