## Summary
Many of Shakespeare’s works use diction that can be interpreted in many different ways, offering a rich and varied reading experience. 
## Significance
This allows Shakespeare to express things with incredible brevity and terseness, embodying a whole paragraph of meaning in one line.

## Examples

1.2.74, 76: Whereas in the first instance of the word “common,” Shakespeare refers to something that is shared by all humanity, in the second instance, the word means something that is of low-value and trite. 

*A certain convocation of politic worms are e’en at him. Your worm is your only emperor for diet. (4.3.22-24)*

Here, Hamlet could be making a reference to the Diet of Worms, essentially portraying himself as Luther, that he is essentially “eating” Claudius, i.e., destroying him with his word play and argumentation. It is just a clever clever dig at Claudius that makes sense on both a metaphorical level and a religious level.
