## Summary
Aristotle rejects the idea of a uniform “goodness.” He argues that goodness is a heterogenous concept whereby there is not a common characteristic corresponding to one idea.
## Significance
With this idea, Aristotle grounds ethics. Goodness is no longer some abstract Idea or Form. Rather, it is something actionable.
## Argument
We first must separate the idea of goodness into things that are *good in an of themselves,* and things that are *good as a means to secure something that is good in an of itself*.
Here, only the former is examined, since the second sense of goodness is not the sense that we ought to strive for in life, i.e., the second sense of goodness is contingent and lower in importance.
With respect to the first kind of good then, if there is one overarching Platonic Idea of “goodness” that underly all concepts we consider good (e.g., honor, intelligence, etc.), then there must be some kind of similarity between these in how they all seem to approach the same concept of “goodness.” Yet this does not seem to be the case.
Therefore, we must conclude that goodness is different for each of its incarnations, and all of its incarnations do not approach on some central, underlying concept of “goodness.” Rather, goodness is something that changes and adapts to the specific concepts that it is currently being applied to.
## Note
In this section of the text (specifically, 1096b26) Aristotle raise the question of for what reason, if any, can all these different concepts be called “good” given that they do not all approach on a central concept of “goodness.” He does not address this issue explicitly in the rest of his philosophy.
However, Aristotle does define a broad conception of goodness: “an activity of the soul that is in accordance with virtue.” This could be put forth as a solution to the problem presented above, as while it is not a strong, Platonic definition of good, it does bring a sense of unity to the concept of goodness.
His argument for such a definition is as follows:
1. The function of a person is an activity of the soul in accordance with a rational principle.
2. A good human is a rational human.
3. Something done well is done according to its excellence.
$\therefore$ What is good is an activity that is in accordance with virtue.
