## Summary
To be magnanimous means to have every other virtue. Additionally, it means knowing that you are virtuous in every way, and acting in an apt manner. The magnanimous person cares (in the lightest sense of the word) only for honor.
## Significance
This is the most interesting of Aristotle’s virtues, since it is the encapsulation of all other virtues in one.
## Note
The deficient vice of magnanimity is being pusillanimous, and the excessive vice is being conceitful.
The magnanimous man does not particularly care about honor, though he accepts honors from good people (since it is what he ought to receive), but shuns honors from lower people (since they are irrelevant to him). He views external events as beneath his dignity, and thus does not react irrationality when circumstances outside his control falter.
Additionally, to achieve this virtue means having access to power, good birth, and wealth. However, power and wealth only matter to him because of the honor that are associated with having them.
