## Summary
The difference principle expresses a social condition that is similar to reciprocity, i.e., a principle of mutual benefit.
## Significance
This ensures that the difference principle is publicly justifiable to everyone.
## Explanation
Given that there are two individuals, $A$ and $B$, where $A$ is better-off than $B$,
1. $B$ accepts being worse-off than $A$ since the difference principle ensures that $A$ being better-off helps him the most.
2. $A$ accepts helping $B$ since 
	1. his being better-off is a result of the social institutions which exist, which $B$ is a part of, and 
	2. the idea that $A$ deserves his position presupposes a cooperative scheme rather than just justify it. One cannot appeal to desert to resist the difference principle, because desert only operates after the basic structure is already justified.
