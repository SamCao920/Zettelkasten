## Summary
Treasury Inflation Protected Securities are generally not an accurate proxy for the real interest rate.
## Significance
This explains a common misconception regarding TIPS.
## Explanation
The common reasoning is that since TIPS are adjusted for inflation, that would mean that it represents the true, real interest rate of the government. However, this logic is flawed because of two factors:
1. Large institutional investors (e.g., pension funds) are often forced by the government to buy TIPS, which artificially drives down the yield.
2. Investors typically demand a liquidity premium on TIPS, since they are harder to transact than other bonds. 
These two factors combined means that, depending on which factor is stronger at a particular moment, they could radically misrepresent the true real interest rate.
