## Summary
The difference between culture and institutions is threefold: culture arises naturally, while institutions are formed through deliberately constructed systems; cultures unconsciously shape people, while institutions function through formal rules; and cultures are hard to transplant, while institutions can be copied or imposed.
## Significance
This is crucial, because the difference between the two concepts is often forgotten, or ignored. Understanding the difference between the two concepts is also crucial for understanding Huntington’s thesis regarding Cultural Determinism.
## Explanation
- Cultures can influence institutions, but the way institutions develop is not solely determined by culture.
	- The same cultural background can develop into vastly different institutions.
		- Calvinism: pillarization in the Dutch Republic, autocracy in the Prussian state, and Apartheid in South America.
	- The Athenian institution of democracy has spread and become a global phenomenon, but Athenian culture has not.
		- Democracy was also not something that emerged out of Athenian culture. It emerged as a reaction against Athenian culture at the time.
