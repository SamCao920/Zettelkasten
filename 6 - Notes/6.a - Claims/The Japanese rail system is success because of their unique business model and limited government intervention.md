## Summary
The Japanese model of railroads have been particularly successful not because of culture specifically, but because of the unique business model of the Japanese rail companies, and the restraint of the government when it comes to regulation.
## Significance
This can serve as an example other nations would follow when it comes to creating a well-ordered rail system.
## Explanation
- The success of rail in Japan doesn't have to do with culture.
- On the national scale, there are six large private rail networks as a part of the Japanese Rail (JR) system.
- They all have a unique business model where they don't just operate trains. They also have a bunch of side businesses that generate a lot of revenue. In fact, the railroad business (although it is almost always profitable) usually generates only a plurality or small majority of the revenue.
    - They essentially serve as city builders, where they develop rural land with train lines and houses and stuff (that they construct), and thus (1) make the land valuable, (2) provide a guaranteed consumer base for their train, and (3) provide from real estate and other businesses that they then run.
    - To make this work they also have smart land use where a lot of the land was reserved for parks and forests and stuff, to increase living quality.
- Railroads generally work the best in areas of high urban density, since they have high throughput.
- Overall, Japanese cities are not actually as dense as their European counterparts or cities in Korea.
    - What makes them unique is their hyperdense centers.
- What allowed for the development of this is their liberal development control systems compared to the West.
    - It is really easy to do land adjustment in Japan, since all it takes is that 2/3 of residents and landowners agree, and they can replan the land.
- There is not an implicit subsidy for car ownership in Japan like in the US.
    - Japan has private parking (people can't park on public land), and someone who buys a car must first prove that they have private land where they can park their car.
- Vertical integration of the Japanese train companies ensures that companies are run efficiently.
- The generous price cap on train fares ensure that companies actually make a profit, and thus actually have an incentive to maximize ridership.
- They can also make their own decisions about timetables, service patterns, and day-to-day operations.
    - In other countries, this is inefficiently done by government.
