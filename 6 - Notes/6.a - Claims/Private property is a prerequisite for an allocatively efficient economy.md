## Summary
This describes Mises’ Economic Calculation Problem, which states that without a market price (and thus private property) for capital goods, rational economic planning is impossible, and resources will not be optimally allocated. 
## Significance
This is one of the most fundamental arguments against planned economic systems, since they face this problem of being unable to achieve allocative efficiency.
## Explanation
Prices reflect the scarcity of a resource, reflect demand for a product, and allow for the calculation of profit and loss. All these things allow people to understand if resources are being effectively allocated, and prevent waste.
Prices allow you to compare things that are normally incomparable (3 hours of labor vs. 1 ton of steel), and send signals through markets about what people actually want.
Prices also allow for the calculation of profit and loss, which can be used to determine if something creates or destroys value.
