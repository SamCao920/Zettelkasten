## Summary
Rawls' view of justice, he claims, would be accepted by free, rational, and prudential persons from his conception of the original position.
## Significance
This adds a contractarian element to Rawls' Justice as Fairness theory, which makes it vulnerable to critiques of those views.
## Explantion
Rawl's view of the original position most prominently includes the veil of ignorance, which ensures fairness in the original position.
The fact that this is fair results in the moral payoff of his system, which is that agents which are a part of the system mutually cooperate and engage with each other under the understanding that they were free and equal persons whose relations with respect to each other were fair.
- In this way, the society is almost voluntary, since it free and rational agents would indeed accept such a society based around justice as fairness.
