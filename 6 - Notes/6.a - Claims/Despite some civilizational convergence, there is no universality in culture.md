## Summary
Although some aspects of the world have become more similar, on the whole, civilizational differences still define us more than similarities.
## Significance
This refutes the idea that there is some universal cultural element to humanity.
## Explanation
- The spread of Western consumption patterns and pop culture does not relate to the creation of a universal civilization.
	- The spread of Western media does not either:
		- There is no data for how widespread it actually is.
		- Different cultures could interpret the same Western media in different ways.
- Language has also not promoted the creation of a universal culture.
	- English facilitates *inter-cultural* exchange, but this does not means that there is a universal civilization.
	- The frequency of a language’s use is also tied to the relative ability of the mother nation to exert control, and the West (specifically the US)’s control of this is slipping.
- Greater commercialization and trade across cultures does not necessarily mean the creation of a universal civilization.
	- Economic interdependence could either by peace-inducing or war-inducing.
- Modern civilization could be more similar to each other than traditional ones, however:
	- There is shared technology and fast exchange of ideas, information, and trends.
	- It is industrial, rather than agricultural, meaning that geography plays less of a role in determining the way a state is (and thus causing differences between states).
