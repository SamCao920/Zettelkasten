## Summary
The rate of pharmaceutical innovation and green energy innovation has slowed, producing what is known as “Eroom’s Law” (reverse of Moore’s Law).
## Significance
This is a potentially dangerous trend, because it means that productivity growth in the pharmaceutical sector is slowing.
## Explanation
There are four causes for specifically pharmacuticals:
1. Increasing competition from generic brand drugs.
	1. It is difficult for newly created drugs to compete effectively against generic brand drugs that often achieve the same purpose, but are priced far lower.
2. Increasingly stricter regulations.
	1. Over time, the regulation surrounding the development of new drugs accumulate and the barrier becomes more difficult to overcome, since there are now higher standards.
3. An increasing amount of money is being spent.
	1. In a stochastic process, more money does not mean greater efficiency.
4. A bias towards “reductionism.”
	1. A trend towards looking at things through molecular perspective, rather than a more more macro perspective. But this doesn’t really translate to the creation of new drugs.
Ultimately, it comes down to the fact that there are increasing restrictions on the ability of firms to innovate.
