## Summary
The Savings and Loans Crisis was caused by interest shocks, risky investments by the thrifts, and the slowness to act of regulatory institutions.
## Significance
The failure of the Savings and Loan Crisis can teach us something about how to ensure that a market does not fail do to external pressure, and how to regulate markets well.
## Explanation
The surge in inflation during the late 1970s led the Fed to raise rates significantly, which meant that thrift had to pay much higher rates, driving their profit margins into the ground.
Deregulation during the period also led to the thrifts making much more risky and speculative investments, which meant that they were at a higher chance of failure when their investments failed.
The regulatory institutions also failed in their attempt to ensure the stability of the market, as they were underfunded and bureaucratic, slow to update regulation to adapt to changing circumstances.
