## Summary
A social truth is one which is able to the challenged by others. A “truth,” even if its actually true, which is never (able to be) challenged is a sort of “accidental” truth.
## Significance
This explains one of the important functions of freedom of expression.
## Explanation
Therefore, one of the important functions of freedom of expression is the creation of *robust social truths*, since freedom of expression encourages people to challenge “accidental truths.”
An idea/system of thought which goes unchallenged “rots” over time and loses the its soul/spark. Eventually, the disciples to the thought mere begin to rote memorize the tenants of the thought and all vitality is lost.
This is also why it is typically better to learn from the real world than just from textbooks or teachers, since the real world does not merely offer strawman versions of alternative truths, but is filled with people who vehemently defend their alternative visions.
## Note
There are *logical truths* which do not need to be challenged in order to be proven to be true. However, these truths are merely products of deduction and logic, and are not the type of social truths discussed here.
