## Summary
It is often claimed that the high taxes of the 1950s was what enabled the explosive growth during the period. However, this is in fact not true. While on paper, the top marginal tax rate was around 91%, the actual effective tax rate was much higher.
## Significance
This dispels a common misconception that there were incredibly high tax rates during the 1950s. 
## Rent-seeking behavior
- People began to pay for luxury goods with company funds, since it was a fully deductible expense for the company and tax-free for the individual. This also created a culture whereby business decisions were influenced by their tax-deductibility rather than their economic merit.
- Executives began to be paid with stock options, since the capital gains tax rate is far lower than the income tax rate.
- Corporations were created simply as a way of manipulating the tax system, so that people pay the lower capital gains tax, rather than the far higher income tax rate.
- Lobbying commenced in an attempt to reduce the tax rate of specific groups, most infamously, the Oil Depletion Allowance, where oil men were allowed to deduct 27.5% of their gross income from taxes.
## Effective Tax Rate
- The top 0.1% of individuals paid, on average during that period, an effective federal, state, and local tax rate of ~52%.
- The top 1% of individuals paid, on average during that period, an effective federal, state, and local tax rate of ~52%.
