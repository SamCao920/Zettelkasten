## Summary
At core of Mahanian naval strategy is that naval power translates into national power, i.e., who controls the seas is able to exert heavy influence on other nations. This dictates, then that nations strive to lock the opposing side in their ports, without allowing them to have any chance at control of the sea, which then naturally leads to the idea of “decisive battle” to decide control of the sea.
## Significance
Mahanian naval warfare influenced many people, including Teddy Roosevelt, Wilhelm II, and the Japanese military. The core of theory originates with the idea of naval control being the prerequisite to national power.

## Explaination
Naval power is the primary determinant for national power as control of the seas allows for isolation of the enemy from their allies, from supplies, and from strategic maneuvers involving the sea.
This idea of the preeminence of naval strategy naturally leads to Mahan’s concept of “decisive battle,” where through a single decisive battle, control of the sea, and therefore and in large, the nature of the war, would be decided.
