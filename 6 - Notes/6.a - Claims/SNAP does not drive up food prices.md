## Summary
SNAP does not raise food prices, as some on the right have claimed. While typically a subsidized good would lead to increased demand for the product, and thus drive up the cost, this is not the case for SNAP as a result of the way the program is structured.
## Significance
This is a misconception that many have who argued against the efficacy of food stamps.
## Explanation
While typically, subsidies lead to higher prices since they raise demand for something, this is not the case of SNAP. Or, at least, SNAP does not have an effect on the economy that a normal transfer payment would not.
![[Pasted image 20251102210654.png]]
This is because SNAP essentially makes food free to purchase up to $300. So, although households may spend some of the surplus from the $300 on further food items, it may also be spent on other items. In the end, this means that food stamps simply have an effect on the economy that any transfer payment would.
