## Summary
This describes the base rate fallacy, whereby we forgot the “base rate” or “natural probability” in the entire population of some event occurring, and thereby overestimate or underestimate the chance of that occurrence.
## Significance
This is a very common statistical/logical fallacy.
## Example
If there is a medical test that can tell, with 99% accuracy, whether you have disease A, and only one percent of the population of 10000 has the disease, it means that:
- In the population of the people actually with the disease (100 people), almost no one will be misidentified.
- In the entire population of the people without the disease, 99 people will be misidentified.
So, if you test positive, there is only a ~50% chance that you actually have the disease.
