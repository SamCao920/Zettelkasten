## Summary
Aristotle says that acting virtuously should not be painful (so it should at least be painless), and in many cases, it would actually be pleasurable.
## Significance
This is an interesting part of Aristotle philosophy because it seems contradictory, as he names some virtuous that are painful.
## Note
It seems that the concept of “pain” is somewhat complicated in the case of Aristotle’s ethics. Although not formally stated, there is a rough bifurcation possible when it comes to examining what he means by “pain.”
1. Physical pain: as the name suggests.
2. Psychological pain: negative mental states, such as distress, sorrow, grief, fear, shame, etc. 
When Aristotle states that virtues are not painful, he is approximately referring to the psychological definition of pain.
