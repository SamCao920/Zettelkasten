## Summary
When a country hits its fiscal limit, it can suffer a range of negative consequences, including reduced market access, higher borrowing costs, currency depreciation, capital flight, or even economic contraction or default.
## Significance
This shows why nations really do not want to hit the fiscal limit.
## Consequences
The consequences for hitting the fiscal limit is high. It could be any of the following:
- **Reduced market access**: investors buy less government debt or demand shorter term debt. It reduces the ability for the government to borrow/spend.
- **Higher borrowing costs**: markets demand greater interest on government debt since there is a higher perceived risk of default or inflation.
- **Currency depreciation**: the national currency is less demanded due to the high risk of government default. This raises the cost of foreign debt and imported goods.
- **Capital flight**: investors want to pull out their assets due to the high perceived risk of government default.
All these could further contribute to economics contraction, or even government default.
