## Summary
When the minimum efficient scale (MES) is relatively large relative to the overall demand, there is likely to be high concentration. On the other hand, when it is relatively small, there is unlikely to be high concentration.
## Significance
By looking at the MES, we are able to predict roughly how concentrated an industry is, which may be useful.
## Examples
**Small MES**
If the overall demand for bread in a city is 200,000 loaves in a month, and a bakery is able to produce 200 loaves in a month at its MES, then, since 200 is a relatively small proportion of 200,000, there is likely perfect or near perfect competition in the industry.

**Large MES**
If the overall demand for diamonds is 200 per month in a city, and a single jewelry store is able to produce 150 diamonds per month at its MES, since 150 is a relatively large proportion of 200, the market is likely to be highly concentrated.
