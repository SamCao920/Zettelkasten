## Summary
While it is commonly claimed that low-income individuals are more likely to suffer from subjective well-being and mental health deficiencies, a recent meta-study has shown that this is not the case. There is no correlation between inequality and mental health being undermined. 
## Significance
This challenges the common view that inequality contributes to greater mental health problems and lower subjective well-being.
## Note
This does not mean that low-income individuals do not always experience greater mental health problems and lower subjective well-being. It is simply stating that the link between these measures are not as clear and robust as one might initially think.
