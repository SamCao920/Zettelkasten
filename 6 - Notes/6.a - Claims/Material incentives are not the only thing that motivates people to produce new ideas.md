## Summary
Material incentives are only part of the reason that people may produce new ideas. Other large justifications for the production of new ideas include scientific curiosity, as well as a general desire to benefit humanity.
## Significance
This is a common argument when attempting to point out flaws in the patent system.

## Explaination
I think that this is true to a certain degree. However, I think that this is more so true in the past than today. In the past, often those who were able to conduct high-level/cutting edge scientific research were singular, wealthy people who had time on their hands. Today, not only do new scientific breakthrough take more equipment, they also often require large teams of people working in tandem. The high cost of equipment and the difficulty of uniting the motivations of many people (who are working for a wage, not for leisure) means that financial incentives would far outweigh other incentives in the academic environment today.
