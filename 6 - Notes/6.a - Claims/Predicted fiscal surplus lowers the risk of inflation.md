## Summary
The idea that when there is predicted to be a fiscal surplus, the risk of inflation is lowered. 
## Significance
This explain a mechanism by which inflation is controlled.
## Explanation
This phenomenon is true for several reasons:
1. There is overall less spending in the economy. When there is a surplus, it means that either government spending decreased, or tax rates increased. Either way, the total amount of money being spent in an economy is decreased, meaning that inflation is slowed.
2. When governments run fiscal surpluses, investors and the public view the government’s currency as backed by real future assets, i.e., that the government won’t have to print future money to pay off its debts. This makes the currency more valuable now, increasing its purchasing value, and driving down inflation. 
	1. Note: this theory is predicated on the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level.
3. When the previous theory is applied internationally, it means that the currency rises against other currencies (since it would be viewed as being more stable), meaning that the currency appreciates, and drives down inflation.
