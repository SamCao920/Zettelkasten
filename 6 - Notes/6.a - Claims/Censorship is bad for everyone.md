## Summary
We should never censor someone since it has negative consequences, not only for the people censored, but for everyone.
## Significance
This outlines the broad case against censorship.
## Explanation
There are two possibilities regarding the outcomes of censorship: 
1. either the orthodoxy is right, in which case divergent opinions would merely have tasted and refined the orthodox opinion, or 
2. if the divergent opinion is true, then we have arrived closer to the truth.
It seems ridiculous to claim that we are infallible, and therefore, the two previously outlines possibilities always manifest. Therefore, we must always allow for freedom of expression. By allowing freedom of expression everyone benefits from the new insights generated through the process, not just the people who otherwise would have been repressed.
We can in fact produce a more accurate definition of infallibility: it is not the state of feeling sure of an opinion; rather, it is making a decision for others, and disallowing others from providing dissenting opinions.
