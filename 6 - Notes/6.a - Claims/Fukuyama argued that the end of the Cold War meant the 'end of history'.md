## Summary
Fukuyama, in *The End of History and the Last Man*, argued that with the end of the Cold War, we had reached the “end of history,” essentially that there would be nothing else that could challenge liberal democracy as the final evolution of ideology.
## Significance
This was a very influential idea which has been widely cited and critique in the coming decades.

## Explaination
- This is a very Hegelian idea (Hegelian Dialectic): thesis, antithesis, and synthesis.
- Saw the end of the Cold War as evidence in support of modernization theory.
- In this way, he was also opposed to the idea of political culture being the final form of political “war” to be fought.
- He later argued that culture was still not a major force against the tide of global liberal democracy: - **liberal democracy still had no serious ideological rival**.
	- Ex. Islam does not have a global appeal; it was only regionally powerful
