## Summary
Under a state of barbarism, whereby there is power in the absence of freedom and law, no one is bound to typical legal norms as they would be under a sovereign state. Additionally, they are no longer bound by the typical execution of moral norms, meaning that although moral norms still apply, a state of barbarism may force immoral action in resistance.
## Significance
This explains why Kant would argue that you could lie to a Nazi officer if he came to look for hidden Jews. Since under the Nazi state, you would not be in a state of sovereignty, you are no longer bound by the typical execution of moral norms, meaning that you can lie justifiably.
