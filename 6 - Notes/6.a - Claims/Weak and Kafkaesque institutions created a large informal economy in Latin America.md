## Summary
The economies of Latin American often have large informal sectors due to the fact that they have weak institutions and extremely bureaucratic legal systems.
## Significance
This explains the large informal sector that exists in the economies of Latin America.
## Explanation
- Latin Americans create informal businesses because of the high cost of legalization.
	- But they do pay taxes, since most of the tax is in the form of excise and consumption taxes.
	- They also pay for this by holding large cash balances, whose value is constantly decreasing due to the deficit spending by the government and the inflation that comes with it.
	- In fact, they have their own informal set of system, which, although is not as strong as the government enforced rules, respects property rights, insurance systems, and torts.
- Both homeowners do not have titles, which means that it is difficult for them to use it as a collateral for loans.
- Since the laws are weak, there is no limited liability.
- There is no enforcement of contracts, which means that it is difficult to pool resources effectively, since people can just betray that people that they are working with whenever they want.
	- This means that overall production will be less efficient.
- Bad laws are created (and a lot of them) because most of it originated form the executive.
	- When laws are not just created by the executive, it is not monopolized by one person/office.
	- There is no way for the people who create the laws to be accountable in Latin America.
	- There is no public access to information/public accountability.
