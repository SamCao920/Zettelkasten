## Summary
A good life, as Aristotle argues, is also a happy one. This is because a good life is a life whereby the individual achieve their duties as a human well, and since the final end of humans actions is happiness, then goodness must eventually arrive upon happiness.
## Significance
This is an important argument Aristotle uses in order to arrive upon the idea of goodness, especially as a means of achieving happiness.
## Explanation
So, goodness it the *mean* by which we can live a happy life, which is the *end*.
1. Virtue (goodness) → right action.
2. Right action → expertly fulfills the function of a rational being.
3. Fulling the function well → produces happiness.
Therefore, goodness produces happiness.
## Note
Aristotle clarifies that one must be good in a sustained manner, as being good “\[demands] not only complete goodness \[for a limited period of time], but a complete life \[of goodness].”
