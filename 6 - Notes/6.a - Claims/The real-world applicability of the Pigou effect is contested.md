## Summary
The Pigou effect, which states that a general decrease in prices leads to an increase in real wealth, leading to increased aggregate consumption, seems to hold theoretical plausibility, though its real-world applicability is contested.
## Significance
The lack of the inclusion of the Pigou effect is one of the critiques of Keynes’s *General Theory*. The theory also seems to lack support from empirical evidence. 

## Why?
- The Pigou effect’s claim that deflation leads to increased real wealth is undermined by the fact that such deflation also increase the value of debts, which can counteract its effect in increasing wealth.
- In Japan, its sustained deflationary crisis was not solved through the Pigou effect, as during the liquidity crisis, people hoarded their money instead of spending it, as the increased real wealth suggested by the Pigou effect would imply that they would have.
