## Summary
Moral mastery, the ability to choose when one is faced with good and evil, isn’t determined when one is born. It is something that one develops through time or otherwise acquires by choice.
## Significance
This is a common idea in philosophy and literature.
## *East of Eden*
The idea of *timshel* or “Thou Mayest” is present throughout the book and it is something that the characters consistently struggle with. 
- Adam struggles with the ability to overcome his love for Cathy and love on, until he finally confronts her and stands his ground against her.
- This is an idea that Samuel Hamilton espouses during his discussion of naming Adam’s children.
	- He says that we are not determined by our birth, and we are able to shape who we become.
- Lee also goes through this process, abandoning her use of pidgin as a means of protecting himself and actively beings to help the Trask family.
	- He reveals intimate details about his family past in order to help Adam embrace *timshel*.
## *The Brothers Karamzov*
The idea of “active love” that is expressed in this book fits in with the philosophy of the importance of individual choice.
- One ought not to simply fall into sin and debauchery, as Fyodor does.
- Although the Karamazovs are born with the Karamazov spirit, they must be able to conquer it and not fall entirely under its influence.
