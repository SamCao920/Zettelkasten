## Summary
In the classic case of the axe murder, where an axe murder comes to your door and asks for the location of your friend with the intention of murdering them, Kant says that you are obligated to tell the truth. By lying, however, the harm done is a formal one, not a material one.
## Significance
This clears up a common point of misunderstanding regarding Kant’s legal and ethical views.
## Explanation
A common interpretation of Kant’s condemnation of lying to the murderer is to interpret Kant’s desire not to lie to the murderer as one that is characterized by the murderer having a “right to truth.” Therefore, in lying, you are doing a “material wrong” to the murderer. However, this doesn’t actually seem to be the case. Rather, you are actually simply doing a “formal wrong” in the sense that you are wronging society as a whole by eroding, if only ever so slightly, the structures of trust that underlie a society. 
This erodes the argument that Kant’s original claim was “morally repugnant,” since it is no longer any potential harm to the murderer that we are referring to. Rather, it is a more general damage to society that is the wrong which the lying produces.
