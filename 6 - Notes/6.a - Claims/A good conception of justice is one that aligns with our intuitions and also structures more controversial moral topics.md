## Summary
A good conception of justice is one which accommodates our intuitions (our "firmest convictions") about morality and also derives a moral structure above that which can be used to arrive at conclusions about more controversial moral topics where intuition fails.
## Significance
This is criteria by which Rawls judges his own conception of justice (justice as fairness).
## Explanation
A good conception of justice should be able to allow for the moral convictions that we intuitively hold to be true, but at the same time, provide a structure that is able to ensure that other moral issues which are not easily addressed through intuition are likewise covered under the moral system.
