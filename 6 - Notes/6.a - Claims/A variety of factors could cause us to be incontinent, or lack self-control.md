## Summary
A multitude of factors may prevent us from being able to act in a continent manner.
## Significance
This explains why incontinence exists.
## Incontinence
Incontinence is the lack of self-control, and it regards pleasure and pain (specifically, an excess of one or the other). 
- It is acting wrongly beyond one’s own control (where as licentiousness is willed wrongness of action).
- It is something to avoid and to condemn.
Additionally, one can be incontinent contingently, i.e., incontinent regarding a specific thing. If this is the case, such a person is not generally “incontinent,” but simply specifically incontinent about something. In fact, we can judge different particular incontinences based on how bad they are.
For example:
- Incontinence of temper is not as bad as incontinence of desire, since temper is rational (though exaggerated), and it is not cultivated or indulgent, and it is “less deliberate.”
There are also two types of incontinence:
1. Impetuosity: failure to deliberate –> acting in accordance with one’s desires.
2. Weakness: successful deliberation –> but failure to abide by the decision of the deliberation.
It is easier to cure the former (if they are not also weak), but the latter is difficult to change. Additionally, it easier to change habits than natures.
## Explanation
Some factors that may cause incontinence include:
- Having knowledge but not using it.
- Having a mistake in applying the Practical Syllogism.
	- This may be because there was an error during the deliberation of the syllogism, which causes the wrong action to be performed, or
	- Desire pushed us to abide by the incorrect syllogism.
- By mentally incapacitated or just in a circumstance where continence or use of knowledge is impossible.
