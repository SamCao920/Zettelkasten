## Summary
This is the central idea of the Fiscal Theory of Price Level (FTPL), which emphasizes fiscal policy’s role in generating inflation.
## Significance
This presenting a contrasting theory to the Monetarist theory of inflation which attributes inflation solely to the amount of money in the economy.
## Explanation
The FTPL is essentially represented by the following equation:
$$
\frac{B_{t}}{P_{t}}=Et \sum (\frac{S_{t+j}}{(1+r)^j})
$$
In other words, $$\frac{\text{Government Debt}}{\text{Price Level}} = \text{Expected Future Surpluses}$$
This means that a rise in the ratio of government debt to expected future surplus leads to a rise in the price level. The reason that this relation is true because when there is a positive difference between debt and EFS, the debt is not fully backed up by tax dollars, so people will spend more in order to convert their money into real goods before the value realizes its lack of backing, causing demand to rise and the price level to also rise.
The opposite is also true. When there is greater EFS, people realize that there is more surplus than the amount of debt, they will spend less to prepare for the future taxes that will generate those surpluses, causing demand to drop and the price level to fall.
