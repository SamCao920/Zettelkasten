## Summary
Claims of justice and injustice are especially weighty, utility-wise, compared to other, non-justice claims. In other words, they are incommensurate with other issues of mere expediency.
## Significance
This makes justice *sui genris* as an utilitarian phenomenon, and thus requiring special treatment.
## Explanation
This idea by Mill is not claiming that there is some lexical priority which must be given to the principles of justice, as Rawls does. Rather, he merely claims that, *in general,* claims regarding justice are heavier ethical claims, which means that they would then be considered beyond matters of mere expediency.
