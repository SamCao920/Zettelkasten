## Summary
The idea that the continuity of existence is something that exists on a spectrum. As we collect new experience and loss old experience, we thus gradually become more distant from our original self.
## Significance
This means that as people's acquired stimulus changes, we slowly become different people, and this solves the problem of "when we become a different" person after some period of time.
## Explanation
- Parfit argues that:
	- Personal identity isn‘t what we should care about from a self-interested perspective.
	- We should care about there existing a future being psychologically connected with us in the right way, in that the future self have some of the same values, personality, beliefs, and intentions.
		- What matters comes in degrees: whether someone is psychologically connected to you in this way is a matter of degree.
			- This psychological distance is the result of the build up of new experiences (or loss) not time.
		- You at 15 and 70 are more different than between you at 15 and 30.
			- We can‘t have the conscience to look forwards to the future, because you will not be the same person.
		- An implication of this view is that you have a moral responsibility for your future self.
	- A good analogy for this can be found in Sorites Paradox.
		- It is a gradation of you. Its like instead of define a “pile” you only define “x degree of a pile”. This is the reduction. There is no true “pile”.
