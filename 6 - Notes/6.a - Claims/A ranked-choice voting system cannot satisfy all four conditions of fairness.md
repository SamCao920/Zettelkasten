## Summary
Arrow’s Impossibility Theorem states that it is impossible for a ranked-choice voting system to satisfy all four of the fairness conditions that he identifies.
## Significance
This exposes a crucial flaw with ranked-choice voting.
## Explanation
The four fairness criteria that he identifies are:
1. **Unrestricted Domain**: voters are free to rank their candidates however they choose.
2. **Non-Dictatorship**: no one person is able to decide the outcome of the election; e.g., if every other individual ranks $A$ above $B$, then the dictator’s ranking of $B$ above $A$ should not change the result of the election.
3. **Pareto Efficiency**: consensus rule; if every individual prefers $A$ to $B$, then $A$ would be elected.
4. **Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA)**: given rankings, the introduction of a new candidate into the election should not change the relative ranking of $A$ and $B$.
A ranked choice voting system cannot, Arrow concludes, satisfy all four of these conditions at once.
