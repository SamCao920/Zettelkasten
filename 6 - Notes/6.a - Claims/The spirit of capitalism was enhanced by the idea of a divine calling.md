## Summary
Capitalism was enhanced out a sense of a divine calling, even if the resulting work is not optimal from the standpoint of pure self-interest, as the divine calling advocates for a somewhat aesthetic lifestyle. This connects the origins of capitalism with religion. 
## Significance
This is one of Weber’s core ideas, that the spirit of capitalism was furthered out of sense of a religious calling. He uses this has a jumping off point to dive deeper and elucidate the precise religious origins of this concept.
## Explaination
- Money is considered a great danger. Therefore, it is morally suspect and the individual must always act rationally and frugally, living an ascetic lifestyle.
> [!quote] James William Barclay, according to Max Weber
> The campaign against the temptations of the flesh, and the dependence on external things, was, as besides the Puritans the great Quaker apologist Barclay expressly says, not a struggle against the rational acquisition, but against the irrational use of wealth.
- When acquisition occurs as a means to an end, it is morally reprehensible. When it occurs as the fruit of labor in a calling, it was a sign of God’s blessing.
	- When this did occur, the money was saved, not spent → this created capital.
- Unwillingness to work is considered a sign of a lack of divine grace.
- Laboring honestly was considered to be acting “in God’s will.”
	- Even if it is at low wages, it is considered “hugely pleasing to God.”
- **Capitalism is the best/most effective way to act in line with God’s will.**
	- This is partly due to its system of the division of labor, which increases productivity ability.
- Inequality was represented as “Divine Providence,” that those who were wealthier were more favored by God. This helped justify inequality.
