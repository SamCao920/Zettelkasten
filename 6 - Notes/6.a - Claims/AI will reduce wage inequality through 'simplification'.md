## Summary
While many people have talked about how AI could potentially increase inequality, evidence suggests that AI could likewise provide something to the opposite effect.
## Significance
This disputes the narrative that AI can only increase inequality and argues for why it will benefit the average worker.
## Explanation
- 21% average wage growth, with gains concentrated at the bottom of the income distribution.
- 26-34% welfare impact (equivalent variation, or permanent proportional wage increase) across all occupations and skill levels.
	- The average is estimated at 21%, and this is lower because AI disproportionately affects lower-skill workers.
- AI helps through:
	- Augmentation: it saves 20.2% worker time on average.
	- Automation: 22% of tasks can be fully automated.
	- Simplification (most important): it lowers the required skill level by 18.3%.
		- It lowers the barrier to entry for complex tasks, leading to wage compression and thus the scarcity premium for experts decreases.
		- This is also the key aspect that causes inequality to decrease in the presence of AI. While augmentation drives the average wage boost, it is simplification that drives the distribution.
		- Simplification means that the value shifts from “knowing how to do something” to “knowing what to do.”
- It especially helps bridge the gap between experts and novices in verbal-heavy domains. This means that math and social skills become the new “defensible moats.”
- Generative AI reduces inequality due to simplification, through generative AI + physical AI means that inequality would increase, since that would lead to mass automation.
