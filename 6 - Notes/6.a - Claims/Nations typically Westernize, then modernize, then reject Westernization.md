## Summary
Huntington claims that typically, nations Westernize first, and at the same time, they modernize, and then they reject the West.
## Significance
This is a major pattern that Huntington identifies.
## Explanation
First, we have the define the West. Huntington identifies the following as elements of the West (importantly, he says that the West is not defined by its modernization, but by its culture):
- It is defined by these values/institutions specifically (though this list is by no means exhaustive):
	- The classical tradition
	- Catholicism & Protestantism
	- Rule of Law
	- Separation of church and state
	- Representative bodies
	- Individualism
	- Social pluralism: autonomous groups beyond kinship groups that exert influence in society.
	- Common language: the West is influenced collectively influenced by Latin.
- Westernization first leads to modernization. After modernization, as the state gains its own power and influence, it rejects the West and embraces its indigenous culture and religion once again.
- Modernization has both a societal and individual effect that contributes to cultural and religious resurgence:
	- **Societal**: modernization leads to the state becoming more powerful in terms of its industry/economy, leading the people to gain a sense of cultural assertiveness.
	- **Individual**: modernization leads to *anomie*, increasingly making people turn to religion.
- Nations today are becoming less Western and more modern.
