## Summary
Suffering is a way for some people to feel a sense of moral superiority.
## Significance
This is a common theme in various philosophies or literary works.
## Explanation
In *The Brothers Karamazov*, this manifests in:
- Father Ferapont, who lives as an aesthetic in the woods. In doing so, he claims that he is able to “see” demons, and he becomes revered by people after the death of Zossima, since he denounces Zossima after his death. This is likely because he experienced suffering while living in the woods.
- Katerina Ivanova continues to express her own suffering as a result of being unable to love Ivan because he is engaged to Dimitri, although she is able to end her affair with Dimitri at any time she wants.
This is the idea of “lacerations” that is present throughout the novel. Put another way, it is the ego, disguised as the victim.
- People can feed on a feeling of hopelessness are vindictiveness because it makes them feel special, or morally superior to others.
- At the end of this, the only possible result of isolation. One is not able to relate to the pain of others, since we are so stuck in our own pain.
