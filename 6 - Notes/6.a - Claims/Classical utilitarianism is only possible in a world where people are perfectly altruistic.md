## Summary
Rawls argues that classical utilitarianism is only possible in a world where people are perfectly altruistic, since only in such a world would people accept some loss to themselves (especially major disutilities) in order that some other, unknown person may gain even just a marginally greater amount of utility.
## Significance
This is part of Rawls’ objection towards classical utilitarianism, because the assumption of perfect altruism is clearly unreasonable, especially in Rawls’ framework of rational agents.
