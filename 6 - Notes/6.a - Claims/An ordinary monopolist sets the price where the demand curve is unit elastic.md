## Summary
Ceteris paribus, an ordinary monopolist sets the price where the demand curve is unit elastic. This would maximize their revenue, ignoring any costs that act as a function of quantity.
## Significance
This is significant as this explains why the ordinary monopolist set the price where the demand curve is unit elastic, which explains why there is often a shortage and higher prices in markets a monopoly.

## Proof
Let the revenue function for some monopolist be defined as such: $R(p)=p \cdot q(p)$, where $p$ represents the price and $q(p)$ represents the quantity supplied as a function of price.
To find the price at which the monopolist maximizes profits, then, let us find where $R'(p)=0$.
$$
R'(p)=pq'(p) \cdot q(p)
$$
$$
0 = 1 + \frac{p}{q(p)}q'(p)
$$
The the point-slope definition of elasticity ($\epsilon$) is $\frac{p}{q} \cdot \frac{dq}{dp}$. Thus, where monopolistic revenue maximization occurs is at
$$\epsilon = -1$$
