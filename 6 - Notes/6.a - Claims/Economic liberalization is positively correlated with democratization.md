## Summary
Economic liberalization is often a cause of democratization in many countries, a fact that is proven through empirical evidence.
## Significance
This represents a third view that regards the relationship between capitalism and democracy, and presents an empirical evidence in support of this view.

## Explaination
There are at least two main observations that could explain this correlation:
1. Capitalist policies promote growth, allowing for newly accrued economic benefits to be distributed to all people in the system. Redistribution does not produce growth.
2. Democratic socialism collapses into tyranny, and thus cannot exist. Economic centralization provides too much power to those in power; “power corrupts and absolute power corrupts absolutely.”
![[Pasted image 20251015072809.png]]
Some argue that countries such as the UAE, Hong Kong, or Singapore stand as proof that economic liberalization does not indicate a growth in democracy, though I would argue that these nations are not truly capitalist, incorporating far more dirigisme and neo-mercantilist elements.
