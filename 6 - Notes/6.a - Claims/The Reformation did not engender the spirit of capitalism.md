## Summary
The Reformation was not what initially created the spirit of capitalism. Rather, it only contributed to the “qualitative and quantitative” expansion of the spirit across the world. He claims that the Protestant Ethics is only one among many causes of the development of the modern view of capitalism.
## Significance
Weber stresses the idea that the Reformation did not create the spirit of capitalism, as it evident by the fact that capitalism existed, and even flourished, in many parts of the world before that point.
## Cultural Determinism
Many today argue that Weber’s work is a work that espoused Cultural Determinism, the idea that certain cultures are destined to create certain institutions (in this case, that Protestants are somehow predisposed, as a result of their cultural practices, to create modern capitalism). This is not what Weber attempts to do in his book. He simply claims that Protestantism had an impact in the way that capitalism had been shaped, not that Protestantism is the root of modern capitalism.
