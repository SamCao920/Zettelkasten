## Summary
One of the major causes of bad systems which, although they may operate very efficiently, maximize the wrong output, is that the competition inherent in the system is maximizing something that is not generally human-value aligned.
## Significance
This is a major cause of some bad systems extant in the world.
## Explanation
Within a system, there may be multiple equilibria such that when competition induces people to make processes more efficient, it merely incentives them to move to the most accessible equilibrium, which may not, in absolute terms, be the optimal outcome. The classic example of this is the prisoners dilemma and races to the bottom.
## Note
I think that this claim may be too broad to be helpful, since some things in the world clearly are correctly aligned. What’s more, it’s not clear that human values are, in generally, internally consistent. People may support higher wages for workers, for instance, and demand that the prices of basic necessities be held constant. Yet these two inclinations are not always coherent. Advocating for higher wages for minimum wage workers could make the prices of basic necessities increase, and so, we are never able to fully satisfy human values.
