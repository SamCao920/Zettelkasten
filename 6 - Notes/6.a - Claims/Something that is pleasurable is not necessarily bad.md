## Summary
Many arguments have been put forth for why pleasurable things are bad. Aristotle refutes these arguments.
## Significance
This note presents some arguments for the moral quality of pleasure, something that is still fiercely debated.
## Explanation
1. Since pleasure is a process by which we reach a desirable state, e.g., we gain pleasure from eating since we don’t want to be hungry, pleasure cannot be an end in and of itself. Therefore, pleasure is not a final good.
	1. *While bodily pleasures involve filling a lack, intellectual pleasures (learning, seeing, smelling ,etc.) involve no pain beforehand. Pleasure is sometime therefore a complete experience in a moment, not a journey to somewhere.*
2. Pleasure is a distraction from actually being able to do stuff.
	1. *While foreign pleasures (pleasures unrelated to the task at hand) may be distracting, pleasures can also arise from the activity itself. Such pleasure can actually be beneficial.*
3. Children and animals seek pleasure.
	1. *We should not despise what nature dictates, just because nature dictates it.*
4. Some pleasures are distasteful, since they are pleasure that is the result of something evil.
	1. *We should be focused on the pleasure experienced by the* virtuous *person, not the evil/morally corrupt person.*
There are also some arguments for why pleasure is good:
5. It is agreed that pain is (usually) bad, and since the opposite of pain is pleasure, pleasure ought to (usually) be good.
6. Since both humans and animals who are rational and irrational respectively seek pleasure, pleasure must be good. Although this does not prove that pleasure is the supreme good.
