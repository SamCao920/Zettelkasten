## Summary
This is a difference between the idea of technical goodness and moral goodness, the former meaning being good at some skill, and the latter referring to being a good person.
## Significance
This is an important distinction to draw, not because there is technically a concrete distinction between the two concepts, but because this is a point of tension in Aristotle’s philosophy, as he seems to conflate these two types of goodness in his Ethics.
## Aristotle’s Treatment
Aristotle argues that a good person is something who fulfills their duties/goals as a person to a satisfactory degree. However, this seems to conflate the ideas of moral goodness (i.e., being a good person) and technical goodness (i.e., being a good blacksmith). 
A potential solution to this is simply to reframe Aristotle’s intentions. There is no explicit discussion in Aristotle’s text that he was pursuing “good” in the strictly moral sense, and indeed, he may have just sought to find human goodness in the strictly technical sense.
