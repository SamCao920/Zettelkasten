## Summary
Oedipus is an embodiment of the Athenian spirit and its historical development. This is manifest through the ideas that he espouses, including a support for democracy, his quickness of action and rationality, his adaptability, and his temperament to almost become a Promethean figure in the way that he tries to act in a larger-than-life manner.
## Significance
By analyzing the actions of Oedipus, we can gain insight into the way that the Athenian thought about the nature of the world.
## Examples
- **Support for democracy**
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=5&selection=36,0,39,13|I thought it wrong, my children, to hear the truth from others, messengers. Here I am myself— you all know me, the world knows my fame: I am Oedipus.]]
		- Shows how Oedipus is coming down from his position of power to engage with the people on a more personal basis. He doesn’t even want there to be a messenger that brings him news. He wants to directly engage with the people.
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=7&selection=26,0,28,47|Your pain strikes each of you alone, each in the confines of himself, no other. But my spirit grieves for the city, for myself and all of you]]
		- Oedipus describes how he pains for the entire city, something that is clearly “democratic” as he thinks for the wellbeing of the entire city.
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=7&selection=113,0,115,37|Speak out, speak to us all. I grieve for these, my people, far more than I fear for my own life.]]
		- Oedipus decides to have Creon reveal the news about Apollo’s  prophecy in the public, clearly showing his democratic nature.
	- 
- **Quickness of action**
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=9&selection=28,9,34,15|Your king was murdered—royal blood! What stopped you from tracking down the killer then and there?]]
		- Oedipus’ dissatisfaction with the inaction of the people around him, by contrast, illustrates his quickness of nature.
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=13&selection=67,0,69,28|I've not been slow with that. On Creon's cue I sent the escorts, twice, within the hour. I'm surprised he isn't here.]]
		- Oedipus had already predicted the correct next course of option before the leader of the chorus even said it.
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=23&selection=51,0,53,1|When my enemy moves against me quickly, plots in secret, I move quickly too, I must,]]
		- While the leader of the chorus instructs Oedipus to act with caution and deliberation, Oedipus says that he must move quickly, since that is the speed with which his enemies move. This is possibly a critique of the impulsiveness of the Athenians, rather than a compliment, since this impulsiveness is what keeps Oedipus from learning the truth.
- **Pragmatic**
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=11&selection=12,0,17,54|You pray to the gods? Let me grant your prayers. Come, listen to me—do what the plague demands: you'll find relief and lift your head from the depths.]]
		- Oedipus does not believe in prayer to the gods as the solution to the crisis that Thebes faces. This is something that Oedipus comes to reject by the end of the play.
- **Mathematical thinking**
	- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=30&selection=50,0,59,12|You said thieves— he told you a whole band of them murdered Laius. So, if he still holds to the same number, I cannot be the killer. One can't equal many. But if he refers to one man, one alone, clearly the scales come down on me: I am guilty.]]
		- “One cannot equal many”; clearly shows the mathematical thinking here and the mathematical language being used.
