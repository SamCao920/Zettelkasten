## Summary
There are many ways of hurting someone through a transaction, some methods being just, and others not.
## Significance
This is how Aristotle’s ethics would decide whether someone is unjust in some way.
## Explanation
The two types of harm-causing acts are not unjust.
- **Misadventure**: causing a harm though the harm occurring was “contrary to reasonable expectations.”
- **Mistake**: causing a harm that is not “contrary to reasonable expectations,” though there was no viciousness intended in the harm.
These two types of harm-causing acts can be unjust.
- **Injury**: causing a harm with prior knowledge and expectation, but no deliberation. 
- **Harming from choice**: causing harm with prior knowledge, deliberation, and expectation of doing harm.
In short, for an act to not constitute being unjust, the act has to have to been done out of *ignorance*, rather than “unnatural and subhuman reactions” or deliberate intention.
