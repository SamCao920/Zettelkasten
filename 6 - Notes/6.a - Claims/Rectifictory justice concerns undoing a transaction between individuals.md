## Summary
Aristotle’s defines rectifictory justice as justice that undos the effects of a transactions (without regard to the relative merit of the parties involved).
## Significance
This is another type of justice that Aristotle defines, and a major type within the class of particular justices.
## Explanation
There are further variations of rectifictory justice:
- **Voluntary**: when people under dealing of their own volition: sale, loan, fire, deposit, etc. Rectifictory Justice here means cancelling unfair gain and loss. 
- **Involuntary**: when at least one party is not consenting, since they are being wronged: theft, assault, fraud, etc. In such a case, the involuntary party is not unjust, and only the party perpetrating the involuntary action is unjust.
	- **Clandestine**: when wrongs are done covertly: theft, adultery, poisoning, etc.
	- **Violent**: when wrongs are done openly by force: assault, imprisonment, murder, etc.
