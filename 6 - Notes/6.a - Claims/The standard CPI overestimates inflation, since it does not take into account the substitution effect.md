## Summary
The standard CPI measurement does not take into account the fact that consumers are able to substitute more expensive goods for cheaper ones (substitution effect), meaning that it overstates the effect of inflation. 
## Significance
This shows a downside to using the standard CPI as a measurement for the effects of inflation, a problem that the Chained CPI measure helps prevent.
## Explaination
When inflation occurs, prices become higher and consumers are able to purchase less of the same goods as before. However, after a certain point, they may switch to purchasing a different set of goods that result in a higher total utility given that they are a lower price.
