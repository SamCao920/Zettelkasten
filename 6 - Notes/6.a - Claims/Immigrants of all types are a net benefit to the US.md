## Summary
All types of immigrants contribute to helping reduce the federal debt, because they consume less resources than native citizens, and sometimes contribute more in tax revenue than native citizens.
## Significance
This challenges the ideas that immigration is bad for the economy.
## Explanation
- From 1994 to 2023, immigrants generated $24.2 trillion in tax revenue compared to triggering only $13.6 trillion in costs. When factoring the amount saved in interest, this means that immigrants generated $14.5 trillion in surplus.
	- In fact, without immigrants, the US debt-to-GDP ratio would have surpassed 205% of GDP, which is widely considered to be the threshold beyond which a country enters an economic crisis.
	- A lot of government spending is fixed, and does not expand based on the population. This means that immigrants actually help diffuse the costs of such programs among the US population.
- The average immigrant generates 23% more taxes than the citizen. This is because
	- Immigrants are more 20% more likely to be prime working age, meaning that the state does not have to pay for their education, nor their retirement benefits (yet, and even then, not all immigrants qualify) and
	- Immigrants are 12% more likely to be employed.
- The majority of the taxes that immigrants pay are hidden, which is a result of the nature of the US tax system.
	- This manifests through payroll taxes, property taxes (passed through to renters), and corporate incidence (corporate tax generations driven by labor expansion).
- In fact, even illegal immigrants contribute to the surplus, since while they have a much lower tax compliance rate, they receive 75% less in old-age benefits and use half the educational resources of the US on average.
- Second generation immigrants are likewise lucrative, since they have a the highest educational rate.
