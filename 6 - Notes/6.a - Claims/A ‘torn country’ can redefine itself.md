## Summary
A “torn country” is one where there are competing dominant culture groups in a nation. There are several steps that may be taken in order to redefine a nation’s culture.
## Significance
There are many examples of torn countries in the world, and this note gives a way that they may be able to redefine themselves
## Torn Countries
Torn countries are able to redefine their culture, given that they meet the following three criteria:
1. Support from the political and economic elite of the country.
2. Public acquiescence (not necessarily support) towards the shift.
3. The dominant elements in the host civilizations must be receptive to the new civilization joining.
## Examples of Torn Countries
**Russia**
- During the Soviet Era, they were united by their ideology and the fact that they were different from the West because they were more advanced.
- Today, there are divisions in the nation between the “nationalists” and the “Atlantists,” the former arguing that Russia should turn its focus to the East/South, and the latter arguing that Russia should turn to the West.
**Turkey**
- Ataturk was able to Westernize and modernize the nation.
- Increasingly, there is a tide of shifting towards Islamization.
	- This is partly because when Turkey tried to join Western civilization (the EU), they were rejected since the West was unable to accept the cultural different between them and the Muslim Turks.
**Mexico**
- It was economically liberalized by Salinas in the 1980s.
- It represented a struggle between North American and Latin American cultures.
	- It has not been able to completely North Americanize itself due to resistance from the general public.
**Australia**
- It was historically Western.
- Today, it has expressed a desire to join Asia. This is impossible, since:
	- Both its elite and general population are either apathetic about the idea or oppose it.
	- Other Asian countries oppose it on the grounds of a cultural difference.
