## Summary
For an action to be virtuous, not only does the action it self must qualify as a virtuous action, the intent/action behind that outcome likewise has to be virtuous. 
## Significance
This is important for understanding Aristotle’s definition of virtue, of what it means for someone to act in a virtuous manner.
## Explanation
For the agent to have a quality of virtuousness, they must have the various moral virtues.
For the agent to act in a virtuous state, they must:
1. Act with knowledge of what they are doing.
2. Choose an action for its own sake.
3. Act with a fixed disposition, namely, that the actor’s virtue must be ingrained in themselves, and not just a fleeting impulse.
Virtue is not determined by one’s emotions (the way that one feels about the world), nor by one’s faculties (the means by which one can feel emotions), but by dispositions (the way that we generally perceive the world that shapes the way that our faculties function).
