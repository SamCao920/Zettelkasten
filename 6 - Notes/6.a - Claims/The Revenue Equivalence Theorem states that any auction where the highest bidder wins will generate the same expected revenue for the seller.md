## Summary
The idea that no matter the auction format, as long as the auction is one whereby the highest bidder wins, the expected revenue generated through the auction remains the same.
## Significance
This is an important law in Auction Theory and serves as the basis for understanding the type of auctions that exist and should be used in particular instances.
## Conditions
There are four conditions for the Revenue Equivalence Theorem to hold true:
1. **Risk Neutrality**: Bidders are indifferent to risk, only caring about maximizing profit. This means that they will not bid aggressively to ensure that they win.
2. **Independent Private Values (IPV)**: Each bidder’s valuation of an item is not influenced by the valuation of any other bidder.
3. **Symmetry**: All bidders draw form the same probability distribution, so that there is no “strong” or “weak” bidder. Every bidder’s values essentially have to have the same range (same probably distribution), and all bidders have to have the same risk profile.
