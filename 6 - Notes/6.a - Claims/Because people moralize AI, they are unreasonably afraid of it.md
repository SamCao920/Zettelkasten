## Summary
People are resistance to AI largely because AI is moralized and presented as something that is morally reprehensible, even though it would have concrete benefits for people.
## Significance
This could explain why there is so much public resistant to AI despite AI having proven benefits for people.
## Explanation
The conclusion in this note was drawn based:
- Analysis of new sources covering AI, which showed a similar level of moralization as other controversial topics like GMOs and vaccines.
- Surveys which show that people would not use AI even if it produced concrete benefits for them.
- On when people were given tasks to do, they would not use AI, even when it would have aided them.
There are several reasons why this resistance has developed:
- **The Naturalistic Fallacy:** The belief that "natural" things are inherently good, and "artificial" things are inherently bad.
- **Sacred Domain Intrusion:** The fact that AI is intruding on domains that have historically been dominated by humans, such as artistic creativity, romantic companionship, and legal judgement.
- **Social Contagion:** Because others also do not like AI, having the same opinion allows the individual to fit into a larger group, overriding individual pragmatism and reality.
The way to deal with this viewing of AI through a strictly moral lens (which even overrides the utility benefits that AI has), is to explicate the benefits of AI equally in moral terms.
- Ex. AI helps reduce the amount of medical mistakes that could occur, saving more lives.
