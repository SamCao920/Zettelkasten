## Summary
A number of attempts occurred whereby the West and Iran were close to reaching a deal that would have normalized relations between the two parties in the 2000s, but for one reason for another, these deals all failed.
## Significance
This led to continued tension in the middle east, and the further development of Iran’s nuclear program.
## Explanation
- 2001 briefly aligned US and Iranian interests in that they both wanted to attack the Taliban.
- The major missed opportunities between 2001 and 2003 were:
	- G. W. Bush calling the Iranian a part of the axis of evil.
	- **The 2003 “Grand Bargain”**: Iranians agreed with the West to end their nuclear program, support a two state solution, and demilitarize Hamas and Hezbollah in exchange for security guarantees. 
		- It was rejected because the US believed the Iran was near collapse, and thus they could get everything they wanted without giving up any concessions.
	- **E3 Negotiations (2005-2005)**: Negotiated the end of the Iranian nuclear program (the UK foreign minister), but the US refused to end economic sanctions.
		- The US Bush administration refused to give any security guarantees or lift any sanctions.
		- Khatami and Rouhani lost their political capital as reformists, and they were later replaced by conservative factions that ended the talks.
	- Attempts at talks in 2006 ended because the conservative faction at home in Iran did not want a deal to happen and lose their power.
