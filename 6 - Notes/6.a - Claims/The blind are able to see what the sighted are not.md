## Summary
Being able to “see” something could often times mean more than simply being able to observe something concrete. It can often be the case that in works of literature, those who are blind are able to see the “truth” in a more direct manner than those who can physically see.
## Significance
This is a type of symbolism that is common in many works of art and literature, especially of the blind prophet who can see much clearer than some other figure of authority who is able to see.
## Examples
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=14&selection=83,0,84,44|How terrible—to see the truth when the truth is only pain to him who sees!]]
	- The blind seer is the only one that is able to see the truth.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=17&selection=71,23,82,26|Let me tell you this.. You with your precious eyes, you're blind to the corruption of your life, to the house you live in, those you live with— who are your parents? Do you know?]]
	- Although Oedipus is able to see, he is blind to the truth, the life that he actually lives.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=18&selection=75,0,81,57|OEDIPUS: Riddles—all you can say are riddles, murk and darkness. TIRESIAS: Ah, but aren't you the best man alive at solving riddles?]]
	- Not only is this line really funny, it again connects back to the idea that although Oedipus is able to see, he is unable to see the truth, only able to see “riddles, murk, and darkness.”
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=18&selection=24,0,29,43|Go in and reflect on that, solve that. And if you find I've lied from this day onward call the prophet blind.]]
	- While again, the prophet mocks Oedipus’ ability to solve riddles, he again states his ability to see the truth, despite being physically blind.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=26&selection=57,0,58,41|A prophet? Well then, free yourself of every charge!]]
	- While Jocasta seems to disbelieve the nature of prophecies, after saying this, she tells a prophecy that is where Oedipus is first beginning to actually be concern about who he is.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=27&selection=74,0,74,46|I have a terrible fear the blind seer can see.]]
	- This is the moment when Oedipus realizes Tiresias’ power.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=33&selection=92,0,95,22|Brave words, and you'd persuade me if mother weren't alive. But mother lives, so for all your reassurances I live in fear, I must.]]
	- It seems that Oedipus has entirely been convinced by the power of the prophecies, even after Polybus’ death, which seemed to invalidate the premise of the prophecy according to Jocasta.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=44&selection=63,52,69,47|You, you'll see no more the pain I suffered, all the pain I caused! Too,long you looked on the ones you never should have seen, blind to the ones you longed to see, to know! Blind from this hour on! Blind in the darkness—blind!]]
	- The moment Oedipus realizes the truth, he blinds himself. 
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=50&selection=44,0,46,24|Who will marry you then? Not a man on earth. Your doom is clear: you'll wither away to nothing, single, without a child.]]
	- Here, after Oedipus has been blinded, he is able to correctly predict the future for his children, at least Antigone.
- During the entirety of the play when Oedipus was sighted, he was entirely unable to even see the true nature of his family. When he finds about the truth coincides with then he blinds himself.
