## Summary
The Antebellum South was reluctant to change and accept a new position in the world after their defeat in the Civil War, which manifested in ways both culturally and politically.
## Significance
This explains the persistence of many Antebellum features of the South well past the end of the Civil War.
## Explanation
This was explored through literary treatment of the topic, for instance through *TSTF*:
- One reading of the book is that it is 3 failed attempts to tell one story is metaphorical of the fact that the three brothers represent three ways the South should not deal with its trauma.
	- Benji is stuck in the past, with no conception of the future. He is literally castrated.
	- Quentin is stuck mourning the past in the present, and this mourning drives him to commit suicide.
	- Jason is able to see the future, but his worldview is incredibly cynical and he sees the world as being cosmically unjust. He is unable to come to terms with past, present, or future.
- All three characters struggle with a loss of innocence/purity.
	- Benji is unable to process this, and thus is the least affected.
	- Quentin kills himself because he realizes such a loss is permanent and there is nothing he can do.
	- Jason fights against the world and tries his best to preserve his purity/honor, but fails.
