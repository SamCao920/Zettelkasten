## Summary
In the past 50 years or so, Europe has been unable to recapture the explosive economic growth that characterized the post-war boom. This is because of the excessive regulation and fragmentation of the European Common Market.
## Significance
This illustrates the negative impacts of regulation on economic growth, as many European regulations, such as the GDPR, despite the best intentions, have greatly reduced economic growth.
## Possible Reforms
Currently, regulations are often conducted on a national basis, which introduces a barrier when trading across borders. This introduces a "regulatory thicket" whereby there are overlapping regulations and confusing laws, adding to the difficultly of international trade. 
Additionally, there is a lack of specialized commercial courts that slow down market enforcement. At the same time, the EU should rely on existing international organizations, as to not create duplicate organizations that only add bloat to the system.
Lastly, regulation should be subject to greater rigorous cost-benefit analysis, as well as introducing sunset clauses, rather than having the regulations remain indefinitely.
