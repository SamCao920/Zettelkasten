## Summary
The idea that is would both be more efficient (a given considering basic microeconomics) and more fair to have monopolies make more money, especially through price discrimination.
## Significance
This seems to address a common misconception today that price discrimination is always bad, since it reduces consumer surplus.
## Explanation
![[Pasted image 20251223113537.png]]
The author points to the fact that the use of things like coupons are technically price discrimination, and in that case, having a little bit of price discrimination is actually beneficial, since it also creates consumer surplus. This is better than the situation where the people who would otherwise not have purchased without the coupon does purchase, and thus both consumers have more surplus, and the monopolist has more surplus.
Additionally, having more price discrimination means that the monopolist has more money that it is able to invest into R&D, which may increase allowing for greater technological innovations. If it is the case that innovation is getting more and more difficult (which is backed up by empirical evidence), then firms will have to save up more and more of their surplus in order to actually make new innovations.
> [!QUOTE] Brian Albrecht, *Monopolies don't make enough money*
> Without price discrimination, the profit from selling the good is not large enough to justify investing in the new product, so innovation does not occur. But price discrimination makes it profitable to innovate.
