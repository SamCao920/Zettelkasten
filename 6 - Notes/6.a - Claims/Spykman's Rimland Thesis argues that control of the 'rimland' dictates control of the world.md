## Summary
Nicholas Spykman’s Rimland Thesis holds that control of the Rimland affords its controller the ability to control the entire Eurasian continent, which would then allow the Eurasian power to control the entire world.
## Significance
The Rimland Thesis informed the policy of containment practice by the US during the Cold War, a crucial aspect of American foreign policy in the latter half of the 20th century.

## Explaination
![[Pasted image 20250922213419 1.png]]
The Rimland Thesis finds several flaws in its predecessor:
1. Most economic and military power is concentrated in the Rimland, rather than the Heartland.
2. The geographic features that surround the heartland can act also as a hedge against Heartland expansion outwards. 
3. The ability for the Rimland to be connected via an inter-oceanic highway makes it just as effective moving large amounts of men, materials, and ideas across its mass as an industrialized Heartland.
4. There has never really been a simple land-sea power opposition.
Therefore, Spykman focuses far more on inter-Rimland conflict, and its implications. 
Through his analysis, he concludes that the US must act as a mediator of the balance of power for Rimland states, especially in the “core areas,” such as Western Europe and East Asia. By establishing regional international organizations, nations would be able to maintain this balance of power amongst themselves, which they would do actively since the regional nature of such an organization ensures that they have a stake. The US simply acts as an outside mediator that ensure no state or coalition in the Rimland gets too powerful.
This theory would come to influence the theory of containment during the Cold War, which would have immense impacts on not only US history but World History.
