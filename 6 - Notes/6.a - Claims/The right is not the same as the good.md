## Summary
The right is not the same thing as the good.
## Significance
This is part of the argument that Rawls uses to refute utilitarianism.
## Explanation
There are three reasons Rawls identifies why the right is not the same thing as the good:
1. The principles of justice (the right) are chosen in the original position, while what an individual considers to be good is not chosen at all, but merely arbitrary.
2. It is generally desirable for conceptions of the good to differ, but this is not the case for conceptions of justice.
3. The explication of the principles of justice must be conducted from behind the Veil of Ignorance, while goodness requires the full knowledge of particulars in the world.
All these principles imply a **priority of right** such that what is right overrides claims of what is good. This means that perverse utilitarian outcomes, such as when a majority suppresses the rights of a minority with order to satisfy their own good, cannot occur.
