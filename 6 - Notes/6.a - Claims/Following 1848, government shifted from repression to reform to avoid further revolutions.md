## Summary
After the Revolutions of 1848, as well as the many revolutions that have occurred both in the colonies and in Europe (1832, for instance), governments shifted their strategy from repression to reform in order to prevent further revolutions in the future.
## Significance
This represents a change in the way that state institutions have approached the issue of how to maintain, shifting from a strategy of repression to reform.
## Explanation
Politicians (people like Disraeli and Bismarck) created social programs and expanded the right to vote in order to keep the population happy, draining the desire for revolution. 
It was not entirely the work of politicians, however, as food prices were dropping at the time and people standards of living gradually increased, meaning that they had more to lose in a rebellion.
Nationalism also gained prominence in the period, something utilized by politicians in order to ensure that their populace stays loyal to the state.
