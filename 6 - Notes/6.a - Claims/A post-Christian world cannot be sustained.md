## Summary
Today's world is a post-Christian one, where the ideas originally from Christianity are taken out of their original religion context and adopted without such a basis.
## Significance
This means (perhaps anticipates) the return to prominence of religion.
## Explanation
- Romanticism was the first form of this, as it was when the human desire to communion with the infinite and real became detached from Christianity, instead attaching itself onto nature.
	- However, due to the fact that the structure of nature is not metaphysically large enough to contain the whole of the idea of the sublime and the infinite, Romantic works of art thus showcase a melancholic and yearning side.
- Liberalism inherits the idea of universal human dignity and the primacy of conscience, maintaining both while not adopting the metaphysical foundations that underly such a belief. 
	- This is why cracks in liberal beliefs began to form in the 20th century and continues today.
> [!Quote] 
> Romanticism is not a temperamental preference shared by a group of hypersensitive poets, though it was indeed vulgarized into that. It is a Christian longing for the infinite surviving the collapse of the ground that gave it its proper object and its proper discipline, and finding nothing adequate to attach itself to.
- Even attempts at overcoming are not successful, because ultimately, there is no overcome, but merely the performance of an overcoming. 
	- Nietzsche’s *Ubermensch* is, for instance, just a return to the prelapsarian innocence of man, a “Christian eschatological figure with the theology removed.”
- Since the end of WWII, the world has been running based on a “negative,” fueled by the narrative of “never again.” However, this structure is not able to remain stable forever, and has already fallen apart due to the weight of the metaphysical meaning attached to it.
- We are now living through the recoil, when Christianity is coming back into fashion as a means of replenishing the depleting metaphysical reserves.
	- New Atheism was the final attempt to halt this.
- The post-Christian world is associated with kitsch, where things become meaningless as become mere aesthetic objects, and this aestheticization is fetishized and itself aestheticized. 
	- This means that rather than actually being aware of the lack of meaning in art, with the aestheticization of aesthetics, a genuine reckoning with what is lacking disappears.
