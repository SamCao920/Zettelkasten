## Summary
The health of the government economy is related to the health of the banking industry, and vice versa.
## Significance
This explains why government fiscal failures and bank failures are often correlated, and how the failure of one my instigate a failure of the other due to how tight the two entities are bound together.
##  The Doom Loop
There are two possible formulations of the doom loop:
1. Due to rising government default risk, the value of their bonds plummets, causing the banks to lose massive value and stop lending. The government has to then bail-out the banks, meaning issuing more debt and devaluing their debt even more. At the same time, the economy enters recession due to the end of lending by banks, lowering tax revenue, making the government have to issue even more debt to spend.
2. The central bank raises interests rates, meaning that old government bonds become relatively more worthless. Banks retain these bonds, which are unrealized losses. This creates a delicate situations whereby when depositors rush to withdraw their funds, the bank is forced to sell their old bonds at a much lower price than initially expected, which may lead to the government having to bail them out, costing the government money, meaning that they have to issue more debt and thus devaluing more debt.

## Empirical Data
It has been shown that banks with higher sovereign exposure with a risky sovereign leads to a large fall in their stock value and higher Credit Default Swap spreads (meaning that they have to pay more to insurance their risk).
