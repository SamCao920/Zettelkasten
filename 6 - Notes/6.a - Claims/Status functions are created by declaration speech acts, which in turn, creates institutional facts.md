## Summary
Declaration speech acts are statements that announce the creation of institutional fact, a linguistic representation of of status function (a piece of paper can be representative of value). This status function then creates institutional facts (a piece of green paper with $20 written on it is representative of $20 of value).
## Significance
This explains the existence of institutional facts in the world, and connects to the the underlying, “empirical” reality.
## Institutional Reality Constructed
Status functions are first constructed through the formula “$X$ counts as $Y$ in context $C$ .” This process is facilitated with a declarative speech act, which is the linguistic act that serves as the superstructure of the status function. $Y$ then becomes an institutional fact (once socially accepted), a state of affairs that is socially accepted as being objectively true.
Declarative speech acts do not describe reality, but rather create reality within a shared context.
