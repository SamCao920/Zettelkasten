## Summary
The Practical Syllogism consists of the major premise (regarding a universal), a minor premise (regarding the particular), and the concluding action.
## Significance
This is the tool that Aristotle uses to diagnose action, and why even when people know that they ought to do something, they don’t do that thing.
## Example
- **Major premise:** being late to school is bad.
- **Minor premise:** I’ve hit the snooze button on my alarm clock twice now, and it is a Monday morning.
- **Action:** \[gets out of bed, dresses, and heads to school].
## Note
The final conclusion of the syllogism isn’t some knowledge. It is the action itself, the action necessitated by the major and minor premises.
Additionally, the syllogism can fail. There are three broad categories when this happens:
1. **Potential:** we are disabled from being able to our knowledge of the world due to external circumstances (e.g., we are asleep).
2. **Partial:** we understand the universal principle, but fail to see the specific case that relates to the universal.
3. **Physiological:** Our passions have physically altered our state (e.g., we are drunk), making us temporarily unable to graph reality.
