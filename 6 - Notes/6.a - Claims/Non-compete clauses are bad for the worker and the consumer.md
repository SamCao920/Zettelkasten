## Summary
There is evidence to suggest that there are adverse effects to having non-compete clauses, since they reduce wages for laborers and raise prices for the consumer.
## Significance
This is a potential argument for bans of non-compete clauses.
## Explanation
- When non-compete clauses disappear, workers wages rise by 3-4% across a variety of fields.
- Although NCC encourages training, the worker does not share in the gain, as the ROI of the training is captured entirely by the firm.
- It hurts small businesses since they are not able to hire many people due to non-compete contracts.
- It also hurts consumers since NCCs lead to reduced labor mobility, high market concentration, and thus higher prices.
- Bans of NCCs lead to knowledge spillovers.
- They are also not any more effective in preventing information leakage than NDAs.
