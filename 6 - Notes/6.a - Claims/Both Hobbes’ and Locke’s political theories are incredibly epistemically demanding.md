## Summary
Both Hobbes’ and Locke’s theories of government are incredibly demanding with regards to their necessary epistemic assumptions.
## Significance
This serves to weaken both theories of the origins/functions of government.

## Explaination
One of the core assumptions of Hobbes’ and Locke’s theories is their assumption of a “state of nature,” whereby humans are wholly unorganized and in constant conflict (Hobbes) or in harmony (Locke). This is already a heavy epistemic assumption. There is no empirical evidence to support even the existence of a “state of nature,” as humans have always been organized in structures beyond the individual. 
In addition to this, there are further assumptions such as that the establishment of a government would actually solve the problems that the two thinkers identify in terms of preventing conflicting or ensuring certain rights.
