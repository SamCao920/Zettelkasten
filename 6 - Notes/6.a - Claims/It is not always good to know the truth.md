## Summary
The idea that someone, while searching for something, lead themselves to their own doom.
## Significance
This is a common theme in many works of art and literature.
## Examples
### King Hamlet
- Hamlet’s primary motivation throughout the entirety of the play is searching for truth, to the extent that one can pin truth as the bedrock of his motivation to do anything at all. At the same time, his enthusiasm for truth is not met in part by his actual actions, as he does not actually act on his true beliefs.
- This is relevant for understanding Hamlet, the main character of Hamlet the play. By understanding what drives Hamlet, we are better able to understand the reasons for the plot functioning in the way that it does.
### Victor Frankenstein (Robert Walton)
- Both Victor and Robert’s desires for truth are what undergirds the adventures that they go on.
- Victor’s search for the means of creating life, to become the “modern Prometheus,” is eventually what leads to his downfall, as he creates a monster. He also dies in his journey to kill this creature that he creates.
- Robert Walton’s desire to explore the North Pole is similar. He is driven by a desire to advance human knowledge, and would have continued on his quest if not for Victor’s tale.
- The creature also expresses this sentiment: 
	- [[Frankenstein, or, The modern Prometheus.pdf#page=167&selection=11,1,14,42&color=yellow|I cannot describe to you the agony that these reflections inflicted upon me: I tried to dispel them, but sorrow only increased with knowledge. Oh, that I had forever remained in my native wood, nor known nor felt beyond the sensations of hunger, thirst and heat!]]
		- While meeting humanity gave him access to many different experiences that are positive, he also found out about his true nature as a hideous creature, something that, had he never met humanity, he would never have found out.
	- [[Frankenstein, or, The modern Prometheus.pdf#page=177&selection=28,12,29,30|Increase of knowledge only discovered to me more clearly what a wretched outcast I was.]]
### Oedipus
- Oedipus searches for the truth through the entirety of the play, and the same search for the truth is also what ultimately brings him down.
- This dedication to the truth is what eventually brings Oedipus down, as he find out about the truth regarding the relationship between him and his wife/mother, and the fact that he kills his father.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=14&selection=24,0,33,47|TIRESIAS: None of you knows— and I will never reveal my dreadful secrets, not to say your own. OEDIPUS: What? You know and you won't tell? You're bent on betraying us, destroying Thebes?]]
	- Here, it is the case of Tiresias having the truth. Yet, due to having the truth,  he is yelled at and blamed by Oedipus.
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=15&selection=18,0,24,45|OEDIPUS: Who primed you for this? Not your prophet's trade. TIRESIAS: You did, you forced me, twisted it out of me.]]
- [[Oedipus Rex, Robert Fangles.pdf#page=37&selection=24,0,34,23|OEDIPUS: What—give up now, with a clue like this? Fail to solve the mystery of my birth? Not for all the world! JOCASTA: Stop—in the name of god, if you love your own life, call off this search! My suffering is enough.]]
	- Jocasta, having heard that Oedipus was not the true son of the Corinthian king and queen, and having heard that he was given to them by a shepherd, begins the realize the truth and begs Oedipus to stop his search. Yet, Oedipus is adamant about finding out the terrible truth.
