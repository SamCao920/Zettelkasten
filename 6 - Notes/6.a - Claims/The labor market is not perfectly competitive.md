## Summary
There has been a misconception that the labor market is perfectly or near perfectly competitive. However, this does not seem to be the case.
## Significance
Many policies are based on the assumption that the labor market is near-perfectly competitive, so understanding that it is not can have policy implications.
## Explanation
- Many of the effects of monopoly are present in the labor market, which seems to be a monopsony applied to the worker, not the consumer.
- Firm mergers should be judged on whether they actually increase productivity and raise wages, rather than just transfer wealth through firing or wage suppression.
- Downward Wage Pression ($DWP$) can be measured with $Markdown \times Diversion \ Ratio$, where if it rises about 1-2%, then it indicates a significant incentive to suppress wages.
- There is labor market power because usually there are only a few employers in a location that require specific skills and there are search frictions for jobs.
