## Summary
The idea that someone’s birth into a situation with any non-negative utility cannot to have been said to have been harmed by society. This is the non-identity problem.
## Significance
This is one of the foremost problems in population ethics.
## Example
A slave who is born with barely positive utility does not have the right to claim that they have been harmed by the people who gave birth to them, even if they knew perfectly well the circumstances into which their child would have been born. This is because their utility, despite being low, is still non-negative, which is better than non-existence.
## Note
I think that this paradox is actually quite weak, for two reasons:
1. It relies on the assumption of utilitarianism, which has many flaws.
2. It seems to exploit our in ability to fully comprehend the idea of “utility.” It seems that having a non-negative utility in life would, almost as a tautology, make that life worth living, even though intuitively, that might not seem to be the case.
