## Summary
Aristotle argues that the rational soul is composed of two parts: the scientific (contemplative) and calculative (deliberative).
## Significance
The two different parts of the soul serve different purposes when it comes to intellectual virtues.
## Scientific vs. Calculative
- **Scientific:** the study of “necessary/eternal truths,” i.e., mathematics, first principles, etc.
- **Calculative:** the study of “contingent/variable truths,” i.e., what to do in a particular circumstance, how to act in a particular circumstance, etc.
It is the goal of both parts of this rational soul to search for truth. What each part of the rational soul does well is their virtue, and these virtues are what allows each part of the soul to search for the truth.
