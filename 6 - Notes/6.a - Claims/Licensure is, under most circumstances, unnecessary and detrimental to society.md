## Summary
Licensure is, under the vast majority of circumstances, unnecessary.
## Significance
Today, over 20% of jobs in the US require some form of licensure. This results in a situation where only the incumbents benefit, thus reducing economic competition, and increases prices for consumers, resulting in economic inefficiency.
## Argument
- It is the case that many jobs that are dangerous, or would cause dangers to others should be licensed to ensure public safety. However, this does not justify an across the board licensure of many different fields.
- Licensure in many harmless fields only result in the incumbents being kept in power, as they can control the amount of people who receive the license, thus holding an economic monopoly on the supply of some occupation. This results in economic inefficiency.
	- In many cases, the board that determines whether some people would receive the the license represents a clear conflict of interest, and by artificially restricting the amount of people that are entering a profession, it represents a sort of democratic backsliding, as the interests of the people are not being represented. 
- A [2015 study by the White House](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/docs/licensing_report_final_nonembargo.pdf) concluded that most licensing does not results in a higher quality of service provided.
- Licensing should only be used when **necessary, targeted (at specific professions), and competently administered (avoiding conflicts of interest).**
