## Summary
The idea that one’s fate and course in life is not determine by the circumstances of their birth/growing up.
## Significance
This is a common idea reiterated in society and present in specific works of literature as well.
## East of Eden
This is a main idea of East of Eden. 
- It is first expressed during the naming ceremony by Sam Hamilton.
- This is evident in the case of Adam, and Cal.
There is large contrast between the way that Adam and Cyrus’s treatment of this idea, specifically regrading their respective offspring.
- Cyrus forces Adam into his own mold, giving him his own definition of a good life and morality, rather than giving him real moral choice.
- While Adam fails in much the same way that Cyrus does throughout most of the book, in his final moment, he is able to redeem himself. His “timshel” blessing for Cal frees Cal from the moral determinism that has chased the members of the family throughout the rest of the book.
