## Summary
Aristotle claims that there are two different types of virtue: intellectual and moral. Intellectual virtues are ones which are taught, and can be forgotten if not used. Moral virtues come from habit (ethos), which means that you learn them through repeated practice, not studying theory. Thus, they become a permanent way that one engages with the world once established.
## Significance
This is crucial for Aristotle’s elucidation of, specifically moral virtue, in Book II.
## Moral Virtue
Moral virtues are established through repeated practice, not just abstract study. The integration of physical practice into moral virtue is its big separator from intellectual virtue.
We are constituted by nature to have a propensity for moral virtue, but their full development still requires repeated practice until it becomes a habit.
