## Summary
Friendship that is based on utility or pleasure are easily dissolved, since these things can be fleeting, and once they are gone, there is no reason for these friendships to continue.
## Significance
This is a crucial factor that separates friendship based on utility or pleasure from those based on virtue.
## Examples
- **Utility**: A friendship based on a business relationship may end after a deal is reached.
	- These friendships are often formed between people who are opposites, since they each have something the other does not (e.g., rich and poor, dumb and smart).
	- In this type of friendship, the value of a service is determined by the utility conferred to the recipient of a service (not by the conferrer of the service).
- **Pleasure**: A friendship based a mutual enjoyment of 18th-century furniture may end because of the participants find a new interest.
They are easily dissolved since in these types of friendships, people only care about their own utility or pleasure, and not anything about the other person. So the moment they stop gaining anything from the relationship, they end it.
- Most quarrels between friends occur because the individuals are mistaken about the basis of their friendship (i.e., one persons believes the friendship to be one of utility, and the other, pleasure).
Truly good people do not make friendships of the kinds outlined in this note, only friendships of the virtuous kind.
