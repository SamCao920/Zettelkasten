## Summary
During times of monetary contraction, capital holders withdraw from the real economy since they are afraid of inflation eating up interests. As a result, interest rates rise, borrowing becomes difficult, and the economy slows.
## Significance
This explains a downside of currency devaluation, since it eats at economics growth.
## Explaination
High inflation raises interest rates due to the fisher effect, and high interest rates leads to lower investment since people are less willing to borrow money when it is more expensive to do so. Less investment means that there is less growth in TFP, which means that economic growth may potentially slow.
