## Summary
Some evidence that suggests that it is more and more difficult for children to be able to rise and earn more than their parents has seen a dramatic decline in the US over the last half-century.
## Significance
This supports the idea that income inequality is going to worsen.
## Evidence
- For children born in 1940, 90% earned more than their parents, but for those born in the 1980s, that number plummets to just 50%.
- Most of this decline was from the industrial Midwest.
- It was primarily caused by the unequal distribution on the GDP growth, not the fact that there was not GDP growth.
### Intergenerational Mobility
- Economists have found a “Great Gatsby curve” whereby countries such as the US or UK, where there is relatively higher income inequality, have lower intergenerational mobility compared to Denmark or Norway, where there is much higher income mobility.
- There have theorized to be several causes for this:
	- Richer families are able to have greater human capital investment in their children, ensuring that their children have more educational resources and thus more human capital.
	- Societies with a higher “college premium” tend to have lower mobility.
	- Rich people have more connections and can engage in nepotism.
