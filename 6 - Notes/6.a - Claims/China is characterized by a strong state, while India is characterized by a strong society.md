## Summary
The historical development of strong state institutions in China allows for highly centralized state action, though at the cost of individual freedom. The lack of such development in India has led to comparatively greater power to the masses and the development of the world’s largest democracy, though the government suffers from debilitating bureaucratic hurdles, local autonomy, and corruption.
## Significance
This comparison between the two most populous states in the world essentially illustrate the two manner in which states can come to develop: either with strong or weak state institutions, with either of these paths leading to radically different outcomes.
## Historical Examples
The rise of the Chinese state begins with the Legalistic Qin state, which was the first to unite all of China. 
## Note
It seems that although modern day China is highly centralized, it is up to debate whether this is due to some innate attribute of the way that Chinese state institutions developed, or due to some geographic quirk, or something else.
I think that China’s centralization is in part due to its long history, its relative power compared to surrounding states (due to its geography), and the evolution of a national identity. So, because China’s state institutions have had a long time to develop, they were able to slowly establish frameworks for centralizing the state. Although there are periods of instability of fracturing, ultimately, the goal of any fracture state is not to remain independent, but “reclaim China.” Likewise, its relative lack of outsider influence means that it has been able to remain relative isolated and develop a unified identity. Today, modern technology has made it a lot easier to run a highly centralized state.
