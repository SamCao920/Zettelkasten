## Summary
An action that is the result of ignorance is only excusable given that the ignorant action regarding something particular, not when it is universal and not when it is voluntary ignorance, regardless of the type of ignorance.
## Significance
Aristotle defines clearly the types of ignorance and whether they are considered morally repugnant.
## Explanation
- Ignorance of the particular: when you shoot a rabbit, but it turns out the the rabbit was actually someone’s foot. You are distressed by your actions, and you repent. Here, you were ignorant regarding the particular situation, though you are not ignorant of the universal, i.e., that shooting someone is bad.
- Ignorance of the universal: you shoot someone because you don’t like the way they look. In this case, you are ignorant of the universal moral principle at play.
- Voluntary ignorance: you voluntarily got drunk, and then killed someone in a fight. Here, you could have chosen not to get drunk, so the consequences of the action falls on the you.
