## Summary
Most famously through his work *The Clash of Civilizations and the Remaking of World Order*, Huntington established the importance of political culture as a driver of international relations, arguing that post-Cold War, political developments were more likely to be along the lines of conflicting political cultures.
## Significance
This marks a significant change from the pervious study of international relations, as the study of political culture had largely been abandoned since the late 19th century and the early 20th century due to it being seen as unscientific, too Western orientated, and in generally, too weak as a predictor of anything.
## Explanation
Huntington predicted that in the aftermath of the Cold War, the various ideological blocs that had formed would break apart and reform based on culture instead (called "civilizations").
![[Pasted image 20251113193422.png]]
He points to several pieces of evidence in order to support his claim:
- The break up of Yugoslavia was very much on cultural lines, and Great Powers often came into conflict with one another due to conflicts between the civilizations that they supported in the conflict.
- ASEAN’s attempts at establishing itself as a unified entity failed, largely due to the incompatibility of the Buddhist and Islamic civilizations.
Later, another piece of evidence seemed to support his claims:
- The Western intervention in the Middle East failed, which is a clear example of how despite Western attempts to establish institutions in the Islamic civilization, due to their fundamental differences in culture, such attempts were doomed to fail.
