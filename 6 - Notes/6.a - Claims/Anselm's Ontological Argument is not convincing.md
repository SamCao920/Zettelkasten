## Summary
I think that there are many valid critiques against Anselm’s Ontological argument. There are some well-known ones that are not included, here, as I simply offer my own perspective in the Zettel.
## Significance
Anselm’s Ontological Argument is a commonly brought up argument for the existence of God. Here, I offer my own critique of it.
## My Critique
Anselm seems to create artificial ontological stratums between things that “exist in imagination” and “things that ‘exist’.” I think this is a little questionable. 
Anselm’s argument that the “greatest” possible being that we can conceive, ceteris paribus, must exist not only in the imagination, but also actually *exist.*
However, this seems a little questionable. For example, take an idea such as “culture.” It is simply a type of concept that could not make sense to exist in a physical sense. Ceteris paribus, would a “culture” that exist be better than a “culture” that exists only in the imagination? While this does not disprove Anselm’s argument, it makes it more likely that Anselm is wrong.
