## Summary
One objection that Rawls addresses is that of the idea that contract theories are fundamentally unacceptable because they represent mere agreement, and it seems that mere agreement cannot constitute a moral theory.
## Significance
This is a common critique of contract theories.
## Explanation
Rawls argues that since he began with commonly accepted premises, a logical deduction to some final conclusion is more than merely a proof of that final conclusion, it is in fact a justification for that final conclusion. It “proves” something more than mere agreement, and establishes a sort of metaphysical weight behind the result of that final conclusions, in his case, justice as fairness.
