## Summary
Under Rawls’ framework, the only legitimate reason to restrict the rights of some is in order to protect the overall system of equal basic liberties or the strengthen it.
## Significance
This is one of the most important principles that Rawls establishes, since it ensures that liberty is the basic principle that no other principle may disturb.
## Explanation
There are roughly two ways in order liberty may be diminished:
1. Liberty can overall be less extensive, but still maintaining equal liberty. This requires that some specific liberty be narrowed for everyone equally, leading to a net gain in liberty for the representative individual.
2. Liberty can be made unequal. This is canonically with the case of children, since they loss of liberty enhances their freedom and allows them to develop properly.
There are also two ways in which restrictions in liberty may arise.
- Natural limitations: there is something about the nature of the society/the place that the society inhabits that necessitates limitations on the liberty of some or the whole.
- Existing injustice: this is the case whereby existing social arrangements already violate the principles of justice. This concerns the domain of non-ideal theory.
