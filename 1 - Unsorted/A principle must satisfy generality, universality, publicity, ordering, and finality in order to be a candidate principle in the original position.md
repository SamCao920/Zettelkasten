## Summary
In order for a principle to be considered in the original position, there are several conditions that it must meet.
## Significance
This outlines some of the requirements that principles must abide by in the original position.
## Conditions
There are five conditions that Rawls identifies in order for a principle to be seriously considered in the original position:
1. Generality condition: principles must be general and does not refer to specific people or require knowledge of contingent particulars.
2. Universality condition: principles must be universally applicable, can’t be self-defeating, and should be judged given ensured compliance to the principles.
3. Publicity condition: principles have to be public, since secret principles which people don’t know about can’t be relied upon in society.
4. Ordering condition: principles must produce a deterministic ordering of conflicting claims based on morally relevant factors.
5. Finality condition: there are no higher principles, other than the principles defined in the original position, which people appeal to.
