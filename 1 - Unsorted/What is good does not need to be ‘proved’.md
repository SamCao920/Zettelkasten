## Summary
Something that is good does not need to be proved that it is good, since it is intuitively obvious that that thing is good. Reductively, something that is good is a means to something good.
## Significance
This lies at the core of Mill’s utilitarian philosophy.
## Note
This seems rather weak to me. Additionally, it faces the Open Question Argument objection, which, although it has its own flaws, is regardless a notable problem for such a conception of the good.
