## Summary
While Hannibal was a more than competent military tactician, as can be seen through the various strategems he employed at battles such as the Trebia or Cannae, he was not a great grand strategist.
## Significance
This explains the Carthaginian defeat during the Second Punic War.
## Explanation
Despite the many victories achieved by Hannibal during his campaign in Italy, he was enable to capitalize on the victories in a strategic sense. In the end, the war turned into a constant grind between Hannibal’s slowly dwindling forces and the Romans, as Hannibal by the latter half of the Italian campaign was unable to force decisive victories on the Romans. He was never able to use his battlefield victories to push the Romans into capitulation.
