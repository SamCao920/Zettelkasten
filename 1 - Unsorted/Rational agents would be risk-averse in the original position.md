## Summary
Rawls’ claim that the agents in the original position would be risk-averse, since they don’t know whether their future selves would want the primary goods, or the amount that they would want. Therefore, they would be conservative and ensure that everyone has a sufficient amount of primary goods rather than being risk-neutral and risk that some people don’t have access to primary goods.
## Significance
This describes the nature/behavior of rational agents from behind the veil of ignorance.
## Explanation
This would essentially mean the adoption of a maximin strategy. Rawls argues that this is rational since we do not know the probability distribution of our future circumstances, so we can’t adopt a utilitarian view towards this can calculate the expected value.
Also, only the minimum amount of primary goods is required to satisfy the rational agent’s rational, long-term plan.
