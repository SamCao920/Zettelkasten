## Summary
The idea that any philosophical debate is fundamentally a metaphysical debate.
## Significance
If this were the case, then we could essentially simplify any debate down to the level of metaphysics.
## Explanation
For instance, debates such as the nature of morality can often be regarded merely as debates about what is counts as a “thing” or the relatively ontological statuses of things which make them ethically different. Even claims in metaphysics such as “what is art” seems to be fundamentally a question of ontology, rather than a question of what “aesthetically” (whatever that means) counts as art. In logic, different logical systems (classical, modal), seem all to have different metaphysics assumptions which is what fundamentally makes them different.
A weaker version of this claim, which seems to be better generally supported is the idea that that all philosophy makes metaphysical assumptions, and therefore, it is the fundamental branch of philosophy.
