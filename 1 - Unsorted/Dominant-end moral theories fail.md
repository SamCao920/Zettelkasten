## Summary
A dominant-end theory is an approach to rational deliberation whereby the result is one overarching goal, with all other ends being merely subordinate means to that ultimate end. Rawls argues that this view is impossible.
## Significance
This is an argument against dominant-end theories in general.
## Explanation
Rawls makes three key points:
1. There is no plausible option for the actual dominant end.
	- Happiness cannot be it since happiness is the result of executing a rational plan of life (see the Thin Theory of the Good).
	- Many other proposals seem inhuman if they are presented as the overarching goal of human life.
2. The vagueness of a dominant-end theory means that it does not actually solve the problem that it claims to.
