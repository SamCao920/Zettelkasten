## Summary
The priority problem can be solved with the bargaining in the original position, lexical priority, the adoption of singular principles, and viewing the problem from the prudential interests of the worst-off person.
## Significance
The various solutions to the priority problem is needed, since it is a pressing concern with the development of any conception of justice.
## Explanation
Some solutions that Rawls proposes for the priority problem include:
1. Using the reason that a principle was derived in the original position to support giving the principle certain weights when it comes to the priority of the moral principles.
2. Use lexical priority, and justify the lexical ordering.
3. Adopt a single principle (e.g., utilitarianism, though Rawls rejects uilitarianism).
4. Replacing this broad ethical question with prudentially consider the moral implications of conflicting moral principles from the view of the worst-off in society, and make a decision based on that.
The difference between 1 and 2 lies in the fact that when weighing, the principles can be applied to different degrees with the resolution of some particular conflict depending on how much of each value is at stake. Lexical ordering, on the other hand, demands that one “exhaust” the application of one principle before moving onto the next.
Additionally, solution 4 is intended to reduce our reliance on intuitions when it comes to making moral decisions, which is a problem with the other three approaches.
