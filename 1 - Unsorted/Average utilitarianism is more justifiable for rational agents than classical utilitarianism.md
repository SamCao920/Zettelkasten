## Summary
The idea that average utilitarianism is more justifiable to rational agents compared to classical utilitarianism.
## Significance
This explains a prominent advantage that average utilitarianism has over classical utilitarianism.
## Explanation
The main feature of classical utilitarianism that makes it less justifiable to the rational agent than average utilitarianism is the population size problem. Under classical utilitarianism, adding more people below the average utility increases overall welfare. In fact, a society with a massive amount of miserable people with just-above-zero utility could be better, in the eyes of the classical utilitarian, than a world with 100 people of substantially higher utility.
Also, from the original position, the result of the principle of insufficient reason mathematical derivation is exactly the same as the utility predicted through average utilitarianism.
