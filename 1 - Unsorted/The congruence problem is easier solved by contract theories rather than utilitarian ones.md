## Summary
The congruence problem is easier solved with contract theories than utilitarian ones since the utilitarian view is too demanding.
## Significance
This is part of the argument for a contract view and against utilitarianism.
## Explanation
There are two ways in which utilitarianism is too demanding:
1. It requires too much sacrifice in order to maximize aggregate gains. It means that one could have to sacrifice, theoretically, nearly all of their ability to accomplish their plan of life in order to maximize the overall utility.
2. Utilitarianism makes no guarantee of the basic level of rights that an individual should have, meaning that if it maximizes utility to remove some people of their rights, it would be justifiable to do so. It seems that a rational person would hardly agree to something like this.
	- Having rights and abiding by the principles of justice is fundamentally regulative under contract theories.
Additionally, utilitarianism cannot account for things like love, which may cause individuals to act in non-utility-maximizing ways.
