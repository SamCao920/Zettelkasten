## Summary
A sweeping family saga set in California’s Salinas Valley that follows the intertwined lives of the Trask and Hamilton families across generations. At its center are the Trask brothers, Adam and Charles, and later Adam’s twin sons, Aron and Cal, whose struggle with jealousy, love, guilt, and the search for identity echoes the biblical story of Cain and Abel. The novel explores the idea of *timshel*—the belief that people are not doomed by fate or sin but have the power to choose between good and evil.
## Significance
This is considered to be Steinbeck’s best work, and one of the best American novels ever written.

## Characters
**Cyrus Trask**
- Intelligent, but rash and obstinate. A liar (he lied about his experiences as a soldier) (19).
	- He falls entirely under his own lie, and by living his lie, he creates a life for himself.
		- It is interesting how although he himself was a terrible soldier, he creates this illusion of being a great soldier around him, and he idealizes soldiers as honorable people (24).
		- In fact, he forces his children to live under this lie with him.
- He has an obsession with discipline.
	- He has a unique sense of justice that seems strict and idiosyncratic.
		- For instance, he seems to love Adam more, since his mother killed herself, and he possibly never forgave himself for her death, and felt that he owed Adam.
- His knowledge/understanding of the world is entirely built upon his understanding of military strategy? (26)
	- He also says how one can drive someone too far, and thus must always leave a way out. This is reminiscent of military strategy.
	- “If you can endure the worst of life, you can experience the best of it.”
**Charles Trask**
- Disciplined and violent.
	- He seems like a man of contradictions.
	- He beats up Adam when they are young.
		- It could be that he beats him up out of love? He is trying to get Adam to fight back, but he never does, and that is why he keeps on beating him up. 
			- To get Adam to fight back would mean that he conquered his fear, and thus his father would no longer love him as much?
			- This would “equalize” them in Cyrus’ eyes, and perhaps that would make Charles feel better?
- He loves Cyrus, and does not like Adam because Cyrus only loves Adam and not him.
	- However, Cyrus does not love him back, and only seems to love Adam. This is why Charles has such enmity against Adam in the book.
		- Cyrus days that Charles has no fears (and therefore no honor?) and therefore he does not love Charles. But this is not true. 
			- Ironically, it is clear that Charles fears not being loved by Cyrus (36).
			- It is also stated that he fears women (45).
	- Charles seems like a man defeated by the world. He offers no resistance to it. Perhaps this is because everything he does is in an effort to win the respect of his father, but he no longer gets the chance to do this after his father is gone?
	- Charles warms up to Adam after he learns about him escaping prison.
		- Perhaps he thinks that Adam has finally “earned” his love from Cyrus. 
		- Perhaps he thinks that Adam is no longer perfect, and thus would no longer earn the love of Cyrus, and so they are essentially on equal footing.
	- He is afraid that the money that Cyrus got was illegitimate since that would undermine his perfect conception of his father in his head. This is perhaps why he is always stressing out about the money.
- He is able to see through Cathy, where no one else has been able to.
- He is like a combination of Ivan Karamazov and Dimitri Karamazov. He is cold, but at times impulsive and emotional. He embraces the base aspects of the world, but is not entirely caught up in it, since he manages it against his noble side as well.
- Charles’ living alone is akin to him having killed Abel. It is also during this time that he gets his own “Mark of Cain.” (66)
**Adam Trask**
- Aimless and weak-willed, but good hearted.
	- He is like Alyosha.
		- He tries to suffer for the sin of others (Cathy)? At least he falls in love with someone with sexually deviant tendencies, much like Alyosha does with Lisa.
	- He is aimless in life, and seems to simply just wander around.
		- He joins the army, and falls into its routine, even though he hates killing people.
		- He rejoins the army because he missed the routine that the army brought him.
- He hates Cyrus, though he has faith in him.
	- He hates Cyrus because Cyrus forces him to join the army.
	- He has faith in Cyrus perhaps because he has no love for him. He simply understands Cyrus as an authority figure (70). 
- His experience escaping the prison seems to have changed him, as after that point, he begins to have more goals and aspirations, rather than simply going where life takes him.
	- He is more confrontational with Charles, and he decides to go to California.
	- He marries Cathy, but pisses off Charles. He marries Cathy because:
		- Cathy is attractive.
		- He felt bad for her.
		- He feels a connection with her (he was also beaten up).
		- Cathy manipulates him into believing that she loved him.
	- Adam projects who he wants Cathy to be onto the actual Cathy, and fails to see what lies beneath this false facade (262).
		- Biblical reference: Eve is created from Adam’s rib, but she is also the one that leads him to sin.
- Adam never really redeems himself?
	- He continued to live in lies, first the lie from Cyrus, then the lie from Cathy, then the lying to Aron about Cathy. 
	- It is the lying to Aron that would eventually (though indirectly) cause his death. 
	- Perhaps he was able to redeem himself on his deathbed?
**Cathy Ames**
- Literally the devil incarnate?
	- ~~She is a little like Fyodor Pavlovitch.~~ She is far more like Ivan.
	- Emotionless and focused on her goals.
		- She shoots Adam not to kill him, but merely because he stood in her way.
	- She is unable to see/believe the goodness of others, because she herself is evil.
		- Perhaps the reason why her meeting with Adam shook her so much is because she never thought that Adam would do something so good for her. This forced her to leave her ability to isolate herself from the “chain of goodness” that Adam connected to her.
			- This chain started from Sam?
- Sexually deviant, evil, manipulative, and attractive (*femme fatale?*).
	- Enters the employ of Mr. Edwards to take advantage of him.
	- Kills her own family in an act of arson because she dislikes them.
	- Marries Adam to take advantage of him.
- Feels a connection to Charles?
	- Perhaps deep down, they are the same. Charles just has an aspect of his soul advocating for the noble, repressing his base.
**Samuel Hamilton**
- Morally righteous, smart, warm, and kind. 
- He is the opposite of Cathy, and whereas Adam is also the opposite of Cathy in that he is lethargic and directionless, Sam is merely opposite in his motives.
- He is bad a making good, though he is a good person.
- He is an immigrant from Ireland, and this gives him an outside perspective on things, and allows him to see things for effectively.
	- He believes in nurture far more than nature. He thinks that people can change, it is the upbringing and the people around then that results in the character of a person, not solely their inherent nature.
		- But would Cathy disprove this? She seems to be incorrigible.
**Lee**
- Wise, intelligent, and contained.
- He puts on a facade so that others won’t realize his actual power and knowledge and abilities.
- He is pragmatic.
## The Naming
- Samuel, in his talking about mediocrity and greatness, thinks that being great means taking on responsibility and initiative in your life, but it also means the greatest sense of loneliness.
	- This loneliness could come from the fact that you are individually burdened with responsibility for others and you are essentially suffering for the sins of others.
- But at the same time, he acknowledges that there may be benefits to being great.
	- He considers himself as little selfish that he is placing his son Tom onto a path of greatness, which may bring great rewards, but is also isolating.
- This is all essentially to warn Adam about the consequences of his lackluster lifestyle. He presents Adam with the choice between taking action and becoming great or simply resigning.
*Guilt*
- Guilt is used as a means of excusing ourselves from responsibility because it connects us with people in the past, all the way back to Cain, who have sinned.
*Cain and Abel*
- “Reject is the thing that he fears.”
- Directly connects to the tale between Charles and Adam.
	- It is a template of a universal story, something that occurs generation after generation.
		- **Is every story a story of Cain and Abel, a story of rejection and fear and anger?**
- Similar to the idea of sin being hell from TBK. We fear confession because we fear rejection by others. 
	- TBK offers a solution for this.
