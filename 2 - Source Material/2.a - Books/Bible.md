## Summary
An important religion text that sets the foundation for religions like Christianity, Judaism, and Islam.
## Significance
Understanding the bible is crucial for understanding many works of Western literature, since much of the writing done after the bible is a response to it or builds upon it.
# Genesis
## Themes and Ideas
- [[Man is created in the image of God]]
- [[It is not good for man to be alone]]
- [[Suffering has metaphysical meaning]]
	- Suffering begins with original sin.
		- In deflecting his responsibility of eating from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, Adam cuts off his relationship both to the divine and to the human, since he blames Eve, and in extension, since he claims that Eve is the creation of God, he blames God.
> [!QUOTE] Angel to Hagar
> And the LORD’s messenger said to her, “**Return to your mistress and suffer abuse at her hand.**”… “For the LORD has **heeded your suffering.**”
- Hagar is able to “see” the well after she is reminded by the Angel and remembers that Ismael will rule over a great nation.
	- This is basically saying: “yes, one will suffer, but with faith in God, suffering is endurable and not as bad.”
- The Binding of Isaac:
	- God’s direct command overrides the other moral precepts that have been laid down by him?
		- Shows God as the creator of morality.
	- This scene is not only a test of Abraham’s loyalty to God, but also a test of Isaac, and his loyalty to God and Abraham.
- The tree of knowledge of good and evil was there
	- To have humans exercise free will
	- So that they could eat from it later?
-  There is a contrast between the Jacob and Esau story and the Cain and Abel story:
	- In the case of the former, the two brothers, although one tries to kill another, the brothers are “enlightened.” Esau does not kill Jacob, and Jacob in a way repays Esau for the trouble that he has caused him.
	- In the case of Cain and Abel, Cain successfully kills Abel, and there is no resolution between the two.
- In the Joseph story, there is a cycle of betrayal that is broken when Judah offers up himself instead of Benjamin to Joseph as a slave.
	- Joseph was originally betrayed by his brothers since he was his father’s favorite.
	- Later, when the brothers arrive in Egypt, Joseph makes them play out what had happened to him with Jacob’s second son by Rachel, Benjamin. 
	- When Judah offers to sacrifice himself rather than having for the brothers to sacrifice Benjamin, the cycle of sibling betrayal is finally ended, and Joseph reveals himself to his brothers.
## Meta
- The use of repetition in the bible is not an error, but rather intention, to ensure that the reader understands the circumstances and fully context of an event, to highlight the relationship of people to each other in the context of their current actions.
- The betrothal scene between two characters is often indicative of the nature of their marriage.
