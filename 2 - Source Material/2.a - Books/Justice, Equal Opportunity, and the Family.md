## Summary
Identifies Fishkin’s trilemma, a fundamental problem with liberal societies.
## Significance
This work was an integral part of the debate over meritocracy and the possibilities for the existence of a truly meritocratic society.
## Explanation
Three core tenants of liberal thought cannot be maintained together simultaneously:
1. **The Principle of Merit (M):** That positions in society should be allocated based on relevant qualifications, disregarding irrelevant characteristics.
	1. *This draws on the concept of procedural justice, which is most well explicated as the concept of pure procedural justice by Rawls. Fishkin directly draws on [@barryPoliticalArgumentReissue1990, p. 97-98]*
2. **Equality of Life Chances (ELC):** That one’s position in society is not determined by the circumstances of their birth.
	1. See [[A Diachronic Rejection of Meritocracy#^precise-ELC]]
3. **Autonomy of the Family (FA):** That parent’s are permitted to substantially influence the development of their children.
	1. So this basically just comes down to a matter of parenting style.
Now, any combination of two of these principles is crucial for a liberal society in any conventional sense of the word. Essentially, the idea that any realistic large-scale modern society will have substantial social and economic inequalities.
Yet, when any two are indeed synthesizes, the result is that the third principle is excluded:
- M + ELC entails that children must develop under equal conditions, which requires overriding FA.
	- `any empirical studies about how/whether there could be the possibility of having some kind of a problem where the state ensures equality through developmental programs that are compatible with FA?`
- M + FA entails that children from more well-off families will systematically develop superior qualifications, violating ELC.
	* This is documented in [@markovitsMeritocracyTrapHow2019].
- *ELC + FA is a unique case, because it is self-defeating without even needing to invoke M. ELC states that all aspects of children that are arbitrary ought to be equalized, yet that would mean equalizing the family situation which undermines FA.*
	- *In other words, such a society simply cannot exist, let alone be liberal.*
	- I have empirical evidence that differences in family circumstance leads to differences in outcome of the child.
	- Even if this is not the case, ELC + FA and assignment of employment according to some lottery would undermine liberty of employment.
