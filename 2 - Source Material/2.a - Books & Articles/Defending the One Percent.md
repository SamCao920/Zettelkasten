## Explanation
Mankiw offers several defenses of inequality:
1. It is not the result of rent-seeking, since there is evidence that the inequality is driven rather by changes in technology, not rent-seeking activity of the rich.
	1. This can be seen in the fact that the earning differentials between skilled and unksilled workers from 1950 to today follows a similar trend as the way that the income of the top 1 percent did.
2. While some studies have found that there is not equality of opportunity, because it is not the case that the bottom 10% is able to reach the 10% in income as easily as the top 10%, genetics may play a factor here.
	1. Parents are children share genes, meaning that even in a world of equal opportunity, there may be income disparities.
	2. There is a slew of data supporting this fact.
`He makes the point here that he believes that there is not a huge disparity in education between the middle and upper class, with most of the educational disparity between the middle and lower class. Is this true?`
3. Much of the debate regarding distribution concerns the elasticity of labor supply.
	1. If this is more elastic, it means that people do in fact respond to the amount that they are compensated and thus the government should not attempt to redistribution since doing that would be like a sieve.
	2. People also have different preferences, which makes the assumption that people have equivalent preferences (which is a common assumption), incorrect.
4. The problem with economic utilitarianism:
	1. There is no way to compare utilities across individuals.
	2. There is nobody supporting a tax on rich nations in order to support poorer nations, which should make us hesitant about coming to such conclusions on the national level as well.
	3. A better way to tax without introducing incentive changes would be to tax based on immutable characteristics, and characteristics that are readily observable.
		1. This would include things like height, race, gender, etc. There are clear associations between these traits and economic success.
		2. However, nobody is support such a tax.
	4. If we were to perfectly redistribute according to a utilitarian model, it would mean that the more and less productive are rewarded the same amount of utility for their work, and people intuitively generally do not support this.
5. A point on corporate compensation.
	1. CEOs are not being paid on unfair amount because they are paid the same amount if not more in private compared to public companies.
6. Against the Rawlsian veil of ignorance.
	1. It seems behind the veil of ignorance, people would have to agree to government-mandated kidney donation.
	2. However, it hardly seems that this principle is acceptable to most people, since we believe that we have the right over our own bodies.
	3. If this is the case, why don't we have rights over the fruits of our labor?
