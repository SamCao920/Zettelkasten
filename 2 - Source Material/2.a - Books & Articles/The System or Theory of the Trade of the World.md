## Summary
An early work into the nature of trade and exchange rates, exploring the relationship between credit and the balance of trade.
## Significance
This is an early work of economics that nonetheless demonstrates sophisticated economic thinking and sound logical reasoning.
## Ideas
- [[A nation cannot permanently hold a stock of money that exceeds its relative productivity]]
- [[Currency devaluation causes unemployment because capital holders withdraw from the real economy]]
There is a key cycle, kickstarted by credit expansion, that he identifies:
- **Credit Expansion:** Supply of money exceeds the natural proportion.
- **Income Effect:** Consumers feel richer -> consumption rises -> import demand spikes.
- **Specie Constraint:** Foreigners reject paper. Importers exchange paper for gold to pay them. Gold leaves.
- **Credit Collapse:** The loss of gold destroys the backing for the paper. Banks/State must contract the issue.
- **Hoarding & Unemployment:** The "Rich" (capitalists) hoard remaining gold. Liquidity vanishes. Manufacturers fire workers (unemployment) because they cannot pay wages.
- **Equilibrium:** Incomes fall enough to kill import demand. Gold outflow stops.
