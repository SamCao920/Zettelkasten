## Summary
Friedman proposes a method of fixing the American health care system through deregulation, gutting of government programs, and returning autonomy to the individual.
## Significance
This is a proposal to fix one of the most often critiqued systems in the US, the health care system, from a Chicago perspective.

## Notes
- Two observations explain why there is both high cost and low satisfaction with healthcare in the US
	1. Most payments for health care are not made by the patient, but by third-parties such as insurance companies.
		- There is a strong incentive to get health care through employers since such healthcare is tax deductible.
			- However, this seems to be rather illogical, as why is health care the only aspect that is tax exempt? Why not food?
			- This also leads to inefficacy: the tax exemption means that a larger share of the renumeration to the worker is in the form of health care, whether they need it or not. If the tax exemption status were to be removed, employees would be able to bargain for their ow health care and get higher take-home pay.
		- Additionally, Medicaid and Medicare made the government a third-party payer for people as well.
		- Due to how there is a tax exemption status for employer provided health care and how Medicare and Medicaid exists, the insurance market developed to encompass all of healthcare, instead of just insuring for catastrophic events.
	2. Nobody spends money that is not theirs as wisely as they spend their own money.
		- Employees would do a better job at monitoring their health care than employers, because it is in their interests.
		- Due to the third-party nature of health care, costs have increased while output has not. 
			- While the advancement in technology is partly an explanation for this, it cannot be the whole reason.
			- Because people don’t pay their own insurance, they have less of an incentive to care about how much they spend.
			- The growing cost that come as a result of the “'pool of money” provided by government health care (Medicare and Medicaid) plus the employee provided health care have led to greater regulation of hospitals and health care, which further increases administrative costs.
- To improve the situation, we should repeal the tax exemption for employer-provided medical care, terminate Medicare and Medicaid, deregulate insurance, and restrict the power of the government to the local rather than the federal level.
- Additionally, the implementation of a medical savings account could also be beneficial. Around half of the deductible is placed in the account, and can only be used for health care. However, any amount that is not spent is given to the employee and taxed. 
> [!quote] Milton Friedman, *How to Cure Health Care*
A more radical reform would, first, end both Medicare and Medicaid, at least for new entrants, and replace them by providing every family in the United States with catastrophic insurance (i.e., a major medical policy with a high deductible). Second, it would end tax exemption of employer-provided medical care. And, third, it would remove the restrictive regulations that are now imposed on medical insurance—hard to justify with universal catastrophic insurance.
