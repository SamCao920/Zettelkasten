# Experimental Determination of Coulomb’s Constant Using the PhET Simulator
## Introduction
The electrostatic force between two charged objects is one of the fundamental interactions in physics, described quantitatively by **Coulomb’s law**. According to the law, the magnitude of the force ($F$) between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges ($q1​q2​$) and inversely proportional to the square of the distance ($r$) between them:
$$F = k_e \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$$
where $k_e$​ is Coulomb’s constant. Its accepted SI value is approximately $8.9876×109 N\cdotp \frac{m^2}{C^2}$.
The purpose of this lab was to design and carry out a procedure, using the **PhET Coulomb’s Law simulation**, to experimentally determine Coulomb’s constant. By systematically varying the distance between two charges of known magnitude and recording the resulting electrostatic force, the experiment sought to validate Coulomb’s law and estimate ke​ with high accuracy.
## Materials and Methods
**Materials**
* PhET *Coulomb’s Law* simulator (University of Colorado Boulder)
* Google Sheets (for data recording and analysis)
**Procedure**
1. In the PhET simulator, two point charges were placed at an initial separation of **0.02 m**. The charges were set to $q1​=+5μC$ and $q2​=−5μC$.
2. The electrostatic force exerted on one charge by the other was recorded.
3. The distance between the charges was increased in **$0.01 m$ increments**, and the force was measured at each interval up to a maximum distance of **$0.10 m$**.
4. All force values were recorded in a data table, and Coulomb’s constant was calculated for each trial using the rearranged form of Coulomb’s law:$$k_e = \frac{F r^2}{q_1 q_2}$$
5. The calculated values of ke​ were plotted and analyzed in Google Sheets. The mean value, standard deviation, and percent error relative to the accepted constant were determined.

## Results
Across multiple distances between the two charged objects, the calculated value of Coulomb’s constant remained nearly constant, with an **average percent error of 0.4%** compared to the accepted SI value of $ke​=8.9876×109 N\cdotp\frac{m^2}{C^2}$.
The recorded constants displayed extremely low variability, with a **standard deviation of 0.0005686%**, indicating consistency in measurement. This close agreement between calculated and accepted values demonstrates that the PhET simulator provides highly reliable results when applying Coulomb’s law.
## Discussion and Conclusion
The experiment successfully verified Coulomb’s law by demonstrating that the electrostatic force between two point charges is inversely proportional to the square of their separation distance and directly proportional to the product of their charges.
The average calculated value of Coulomb’s constant ($k_e$​) was within **0.4% of the accepted SI value**, a remarkably low margin of error. This suggests that the chosen procedure—incrementally adjusting distance in the PhET simulator, recording forces, and applying Coulomb’s law—was effective and precise.
**Potential Sources of Error:**
* Simulator rounding and display precision may introduce small discrepancies.
* Limited sampling (only five distances tested) reduces statistical robustness.
* Real-world experiments would also face environmental interference (e.g., air currents, imperfectly isolated charges), which were not present in this idealized simulation.
**Conclusion:**
This lab procedure provides strong experimental support for Coulomb’s law. It shows that computational simulation can be used to verify physical constants with high accuracy. Future studies could expand the dataset, include a broader range of charges and distances, and compare simulated results with real laboratory measurements.
